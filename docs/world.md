# World building at runtime

Frame: x = east, z = south (north = −z), y up, 1 unit = 1 m, origin at the Ratusz; the axes are not turned (`GEO.frame.bearingDeg` 0, true north up; see [`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). Geodata reaches the world builders mostly through `js/city-config.js` exports (`BLOCKS`, `ROADS`, `PLAZAS`, `LAND_RINGS`/`LAND_HOLES`, `WATER`, `OUTSKIRTS`, `BRIDGES`, `LANDMARK_SITES`, `SIGNAL_SITES`). Only `road-network.js` (`GEO.roadGraph`), `buildings.js` (`GEO.parks`), `city-map.js` (`frame`, `parks`) and `tram-infra.js` (`GEO.rail`, `GEO.tramStops`) import `GEO` directly.

**Land and water** go through the `city-config.js` land raster (2 m cells over the detail box, 11.6 M of them, built at boot in ~160 ms): `isLand(x, z, margin)` counts bridge decks as land (deck cells are pinned 255 m from water, so a shore margin never narrows a bridge), `isWater` (open water; false on a deck), `distToWater`, `waterAt` (the `WATER` entry under a point; use it to tell a deck over the river from one over land), and `clampToLand(x, z, margin)`, which returns the nearest qualifying point in 2-D as `{x, z}` (always a point; callers needing the margin re-check `isLand`). Outside the detail box is neither land nor water. Also `CITY_BOUNDS` (the detail box, `sidewalkHeight` 0.22, `waterLevel` −3), `RAISED_GROUND` (raised rects the controllers stand on; empty until landmarks push theirs), `districtAt` (osiedle name or null) and `SPAWN` (`{x, z, heading}`: the westbound Św. Marcin carriageway by CK Zamek, facing west).

## Map (non-obvious ownership)
- `city-grid.js` `buildCityGrid`: the water plane at `waterLevel`, the street-level land deck (`LAND_RINGS` minus the ponds in `LAND_HOLES`), battered stone embankments from the deck edge down below the water, raised sidewalks and curbs of every `BLOCKS` curb island (instanced slabs for rect islands, extruded outlines for the rest), hedged medians, the paved squares (`PLAZAS`, deduplicated, minus outlines carrying > 30 m of road centreline; see **Tram track on squares** below) and the park blocks (lawn, trees, lamps, ponds cut out). It also exports `batchStaticGroupMeshes`, which landmarks.js reuses.
  - **Square & park colliders** (`propColliders`, not `colliders`): every bench (an `obb` turned with it, 1.9 × 0.54 m, 0.92 m tall), lamppost (0.4 m box up to the globe, 4.45 m), granite planter (its full 2.4 m box with the hedge, 0.85 m) and the trunk of every tree city-grid.js plants (planters, park beds and the part-park lawns: a box 0.56 × `scale` m wide, 3 m above its base, so a jump off a planter can't clear it) is a player collider. Measured: 229 benches, 1503 lampposts, 14,946 trees, 16,785 colliders in all (logged as `[cityGrid]`). main.js appends them after the road network is built, like the street furniture's, so they are neither traffic obstacles nor name-plate walls; the network is unchanged. Park bed copings and lawns don't collide. `tests/controllers.test.cjs` builds the grid headless and walks into a Stary Rynek bench, lamppost and planter.
  - **Tram track on squares and furniture off the tracks.** Trams do not collide with props, so city-grid.js exports `railClearance(x, z, r)` (distance to the nearest `GEO.rail` centreline, tram or mainline, from a 32 m bucket index) and `clearOfRails(x, z, radius)`: a prop's footprint keeps `RAIL_PROP_KEEP` 2 m from every centreline. Square lamps, benches and planter trees and the park lamps check it as they are placed (a dropped edge lamp takes its bench along); park and lawn trees are filtered before their trunk colliders. street-details.js checks it in every placement (signal pole spots, gas lanterns, LED masts, curbside furniture, kiosks and columns, street trees), so a signal pole moves on to its next spot. Measured: 48 square & park props dropped (39 lamps, 9 trees), 154 street furniture footprints (18 signal poles, 34 LED masts, 73 bollards, 22 hydrants…) and 66 street trees. Two squares carry tram track (`pavedPlazas()` sets `tramSegs`): Plac Cyryla Ratajskiego (146 m) and Plac Wielkopolski (366 m). Their slab is cut `TRAM_CUT` (half gauge + head + 1.5 m, 2.29 m) either side of every tram segment: `cutSlab` ear-clips the outline and subtracts each segment's quad (lengthened by the same at both ends to close bends) by half-plane clipping, leaving convex pieces (59 + 136 curb pieces; the paving keeps 0.3 m further back for the curb-stone rim). The rails, laid by tram-infra.js on the deck (flush at 0.016, or on ballast on Plac Wielkopolski's off-street track), show in the cut; the island platforms (0.25 m) stand partly in the cut and partly on the slab (0.24 m). A square with buildings and tram track on it would be left unpaved (`cutSlab` takes no holes; none so far). `buildCityGrid` returns the pieces as `cutPlazas`. `tests/tram-clearance.test.cjs` checks all of it: no square, park or street furniture collider, footprint or tree within 2 m of a track, no tram track in a landmark outline, and no slab over the rails.
- `outskirts.js` `buildOutskirts(scene)` → `{ group, colliders: [] }`: everything between the detail box and the city boundary (`OUTSKIRTS`, lakes as holes), a horizon filler beyond the city line, the land-use "city carpet" shader with per-zone street grids, and instanced buildings on the zone grids. The osiedle zones (Winogrady, Piątkowo, Rataje, Chartowo, Żegrze, plus a share of Grunwald, Ogrody and Naramowice blocks) get wielka płyta superblocks: 5-storey slabs 60–120 m long, 11-storey slabs and 16-storey point blocks (2.8 m storeys + a 1.2 m attic), at least `panelGap` = max(18 m, 0.9 × height) apart and clear of zone borders, on lawns with tree clumps and footpaths that the ground shader paints for osiedle zones. They are `createFacadeBoxes` boxes on `createPanelBlockMaterial` (`boxLocal`), with windowless gable ends and stairwell doors on one long side; five finishes (`PANEL_FINISHES`: grey, cream and three retrofit accents) share one pipeline, chunked at 3.6 km, so each chunk costs one draw per finish (about 60 in all). Villas, plastered blocks and sheds stay unit boxes on the cheap `createBoroughBuildingMaterial`, chunked at 1.2 km. Hand-placed land use is written in lon/lat through `LL()`. Nothing collides: ground modes never leave the detail box.
- `bridges.js` `buildBridges(scene, { sidewalks })` → `{ group, colliders }`: one structure per `BRIDGES` name (links merged across carriageways and junction pieces), as a tied arch, a girder/box beam or an overpass (`KIND_BY_NAME`, else by whether `waterAt` finds water under it). The roadway deck sits at y ≈ −0.01 on the flat road graph and runs `DECK_REACH` 15 m onto each bank; the player collides only with the parapets (oriented boxes). `sidewalks` is accepted but unused so far.
- `buildings.js` `buildCityBlocks`: generic buildings on every block's lot rects as Poznań perimeter blocks (corner houses, rows of 4–5-storey kamienice with oficyny round the courtyards; the Rynek's pastel houses, the Dzielnica Cesarska's sandstone public buildings, offices, villas, works and osiedle panel slabs by district), plus the streamed roof and street-level detail. `balconies.js` is that detail's near/far wrought-iron balcony renderer.
- `landmarks.js` `buildLandmarks(scene)` → `{ group, colliders, update }`: the hand-modelled Poznań landmarks, one area module each (`landmarks-rynek.js`: Ratusz with the noon koziołki, Domki Budnicze, Wieża Wójtowska; `landmarks-heritage.js`: Katedra, Fara, Zamek Królewski, Brama Poznania, Śródka's mural square; `landmarks-centrum.js`: Zamek Cesarski, Teatr Wielki, Collegium Minus, Okrąglak, the 1956 crosses; `landmarks-dworzec.js`: Poznań Główny, Stary Dworzec, Avenida, Stary Browar, Iglica; `landmarks-towers.js`: Bałtyk and the skyline towers). They share `landmark-kit.js`: `fitToSite`, the canonical materials (`createLandmarkMaterials`), the facade-box pass and roof/lathe/prism helpers, with the modelling conventions in its header. Static parts go into the `Landmarks` root; moving parts (the goats) into a separate `LandmarksDynamic` root that is neither batched nor culled. `street-details.js`: signal heads, street name plates, streetlights, curbside furniture, kiosks and advertising columns, and all trees (it plants them through `trees.js` `createTreeField`); see [Street furniture](#street-furniture-street-detailsjs).
- `road-network.js`: the drivable and walkable graph. `traffic.js` builds it; it is not built in main.js. `road-paint.js`: road markings generated from that graph.
- `tram-infra.js` `buildTramInfrastructure(scene, network)` → `{ group, colliders, stops }`: the static tram infrastructure (Phase 9 step 1), see [Tram infrastructure](#tram-infrastructure).

## Build order (main.js `initApp`) and why it is fixed
1. `buildCityGrid(scene)` comes first. It assigns **live `let` exports** that later builders import: `parkTreeSites`, `parkLampSites`, `pavedPlazaRects` and `sidewalkBlocks`. They are `undefined` until it has run.
2. `buildOutskirts(scene)`, then `buildBridges(scene, { sidewalks: cityGrid.sidewalks })`.
3. `buildLandmarks(scene)`.
4. `buildCityBlocks` keeps buildings off the `BRIDGES` decks (+3 m, 4 m past each end) and reads the plaza, tree and sidewalk exports.
5. `buildStreetDetails(scene, { buildingColliders: allColliders })`: it needs the building colliders for the corner name plates. Its own `colliders` join `allColliders` only after step 6, since its footprints already go to the network; so do `cityGrid.propColliders` (square & park furniture and trees).
6. `createTrafficSystem(scene, cityGrid.sidewalks, obstacles)` → `buildRoadNetwork`. The obstacles are `cityGrid.footprints`, `streetDetails.footprints` and the colliders with no `obb` and `minY < 3.5`.
7. `buildRoadPaint(scene, trafficSystem.network)`, then `buildTramInfrastructure(scene, trafficSystem.network)`: it needs the lanes to keep clear of, so none of it is a traffic obstacle; its colliders join `allColliders`.
8. Controllers get `allColliders`: landmarks + blocks + outskirts + bridges + city-grid + street furniture + square & park furniture + tram infrastructure.
9. `setupSceneCulling` runs over the root groups found **by name**: `CityGrid`, `CityBlocks`, `StreetDetails`, `Landmarks`, `Outskirts`, `Bridges`, `TramInfrastructure`.
10. On WebGPU only, instance matrices are swapped for storage attributes; see [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#renderer-and-the-webgl2-fallback).

Per frame: `cityBlocks.update(camX, camZ)` (detail streaming), `cityBlocks.updateLights`, `streetDetails.update(camera)` (pooled lamp lights), `landmarks.update(dt, elapsed, presetId)` (the Ratusz goats, keyed to the `midday` lighting preset), and `updateSceneCulling`. Outskirts and bridges have no per-frame update. Trees update themselves from a chained `scene.onBeforeRender` that skips orthographic and array cameras, so shadow passes don't change the levels of detail.

## Road network (shared by traffic, pedestrians and paint)
- The network comes from the OSM centreline graph (`GEO.roadGraph`): junctions (nearby graph nodes merged), straight runs between them and every bend, lanes from each link's real width, OSM lane count and direction. Each edge carries its link's speed limit as `limit` (m/s; from the link's km/h, 50 where it has none), which caps the drivers' desired speed (`docs/actors.md`). Bridge links are ordinary links, because `isLand` counts their decks. It is then **cut by what was built**: lanes must keep clear of the curbs and obstacles city-grid and street-details hand over. If you move curbs or sidewalks in city-grid.js, lanes vanish or reappear.
- **Lane placement** (what Phase 15.2 fixed, "shifted lanes, cars line up oddly" on Św. Marcin and Roosevelta;
  `tests/road-network.test.cjs` checks it on the real network with the curbs city-grid hands over):
  - **Ports.** A run into a merged junction (graph nodes under 14 m apart, step 2) ends level with the junction centre
    but on the line of its link's own end: `addEdge` takes the run's `p0`/`p1`, and the edge records how far its line
    passes the nodes (`offFrom`/`offTo`, right of it). An arm's `half` includes that offset, so crossing roads still
    stop clear of both roadways of a divided road. Skewed into the centre, which lies in the median or the tram strip,
    the runs led their lanes across it and queued cars at an angle before the stop line.
  - **Divided roads.** Two one-way roadways between the same two junctions keep their own lines; only roadways drawn
    closer than `PAIR_SEP` (4 m) share the junction centres' line with each way's lanes from 1.8 m right of it. They are
    `divided` and major by both roadways' width either way. One-way roadways the partner pass finds on top of each other
    keep to the right of the line midway between them. Paint pairs two edges into one road only when they run along one
    line.
  - **Tram strips.** A tram link is 6.5 m (the strip) wider than its carriageway. Where the tracks run beside the
    carriageway (link flags bit 3 right / bit 4 left of a→b, `docs/geodata.md`), the roadway is laid out 3.25 m to the
    tracks' side of the link's points, as poz-blocks.mjs stamps it, and the lanes stay on the carriageway (`lanesOf`).
    Centred on every tram link, the strip put 3.25 m of roadway past the real outer curb and the lanes looked shifted
    towards the tracks.
  - **Lane count changes at bends.** One-way lanes are centred on their roadway, so where OSM splits a way to change its
    lane count every lane shifts sideways. Both edges then end `1 + 3 × shift` m before the bend (up to `SHIFT_BACK`
    7 m) instead of 1 m, so the S-bend runs at ~8° rather than a 2 m kink at ~40°.
  - **Paint and traffic agree:** stop lines routing moves after the paint snapshot (step 4's `inTheWay`, the re-placing
    after step 5) are copied back to `paintEdges`.
  - Measured on the whole network (before → after): lane centres off their OSM carriageway 1.8% → 0.7% of samples, a
    car's width over a curb or median 3.4% → 2.3%, the outer lane over 5 m from its curb 5.4% → 3.0%, straight moves
    steeper than 15° 7.0% → 2.0%. Święty Marcin: off 4.3% (up to 7 m, on the tracks by the Kaponiera) → 0.3%, steep
    7 of 38 → 2 of 60. Franklina Roosevelta: off 1.4% (up to 6.7 m) → 0.2%, outer lane far from its curb 5.6% → 1.8%,
    steep 12 of 118 → 0 of 124. Still open: lanes within 2.3 m of tram track whose OSM carriageway is further off (1.0%,
    Most Królowej Jadwigi, Jana Pawła II: OSM draws the carriageway that close to the tracks), and two-way streets with
    the tracks down the middle, whose lanes stay on the tracks.
- **Curbs:** city-grid passes rect islands as they are and irregular islands as their **ungrown** lot rects. The 4.4 m-grown rect cover (`sidewalkBlocks`, for rect consumers) reaches into the roadway on turned blocks; passed as curbs, it cut the largest strongly connected set from ~3150 to ~1660 edges.
- `buildRoadNetwork` returns `{ nodes, edges, paintEdges, connector, curvePoint, walkable, isRoadClear }`.
  - Edges are straight and directed. Each has a unit direction `(dx, dz)`, a right normal `(rx, rz) = (−dz, dx)`, and `lanes` as lateral offsets right of the centreline, sorted left to right. `paintEdges` carry `limit`, and their nodes `signal` (set at step 6).
  - Nodes have `in`/`out` edges, `arms`, `signal`, and `shape` for bend points (one road going on, < 35°: no corner, no crosswalk).
  - Step 7 assigns sides and right-of-way groups: a node's sides are its arms, numbered from 0; the arms of one road form a group, and group 0 is the widest road (`axis` `'ave'` marks major roads).
  - Lane-to-lane curves are built lazily by `connector(e, i, f, j)` and cached.
  - Only the largest strongly connected component is kept, so no vehicle can reach a dead end. A blocked lane can make whole streets vanish from traffic. The build logs `[road-network] edges: N with clear lanes, M long enough, K in the largest strongly connected set`; watch K after touching curbs or obstacles. `tests/road-network.test.cjs` pins it on the real land: the bare graph (no curbs or furniture) at > 6200 long enough and > 3850 edges / 175 km in the set (measured 6635, 4235 / 199.3 km; 6925, 4300 / 193.8 km before the ports, whose runs need fewer shape nodes), and street-details' furniture must leave ≥ 99% of those km (measured 198.5 km; furniture keeps outside `roadClearance`, see Street furniture below). Runs get a shape node wherever the real centreline strays > 0.5 m from the chord (not within 25 m of a junction): with bends-only shape nodes, gentle arcs strayed ~3 m and the outer lanes hit the curbside lamps, which cut the set to 127 km.
- **Obstacle rule:** an obstacle footprint anywhere within `LANE_CLEAR` (1.2 m) of a lane, between two nodes, removes that lane from the whole edge. Keep obstacle footprints on curb islands, or deliberately in medians.
- For pedestrians, `walkable(minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ)` rejects any non-curb footprint. `pedestrian-routes.js` uses `walkable` and `blockAt`.
- **Signals:** `SIGNAL_SITES` (city-config) is the one list of signalised junctions, built from `GEO.roadGraph.signals` (235 OSM signal nodes), `{x, z, nsWidth, ewWidth, junction, arms}` each (`nsWidth`/`ewWidth`: the widest arm running mostly north–south and east–west; `junction`/`arms`: the graph nodes merged into one junction as in step 2, and the links leaving it, for the signal heads). Step 6 marks the node within `SIGNAL_REACH` 12 m of each site, and `street-details.js` section 1 mounts signal heads at each junction (it runs before the network exists, so it can't read `node.signal`). Lenses and traffic both run on `SIGNAL_TIMING` (city-config) and the `signalClock` uniform, which main.js sets every frame. Signals run two phases: group 0 (the widest road; `isAvenue` in `signalPhase`/`walkSignal`) and every other group. Each runs green, amber, red and then, as in Poland, red and amber together (`'redAmber'`, the last `redAmber` 2 s of its red, while the other group is on amber) before green; the lens material lights both lamps then, and the pedestrian heads stay red.
- `road-paint.js` draws on `network.paintEdges` and uses `isRoadClear` to drop centre lines that would cross obstacles. Between the intersection box and each stop line (an edge end), `stopBack` leaves `CROSSWALK` (4.3 m: a 4.0 m P-10 zebra plus 0.15 m each side) right at the box, then `STOP_SETBACK` (1.75 m) to the stop line, both exported from road-network.js. Every edge carries its own `setbackFrom`/`setbackTo` (stop line to crosswalk band, also on `paintEdges`), and pedestrian-routes.js (whose crosswalks road-paint.js paints) and traffic.js place the crosswalk from those, so they agree wherever `place` changes them: on a link too short to leave 10 m (or half its span) to queue on, the stop lines first give up the setback, which keeps the zebras by the junction, and only then pull in on the crossing roads (`squeezed`), zebras and all; where step 4 moves a stop line back off a bend, the setback grows and the zebra stays. Change the constants in that one place. With them, the strongly connected set is unchanged (4300 edges, 193.8 km of span). The stop-line-to-stop-line length went from 160.6 km (`CROSSWALK` 2.5 m) to 156.7 km (4.3 m) to 153.2 km (plus the setback; 207.5 → 203.3 lane km). 2005 of the 3180 junction ends of the kept edges have the whole setback, 135 part of it and 1040 none (873 links cut to the 10 m minimum, 777 before the setback; 520 squeezed, 523 before).
- **Markings** (Polish, all white). The sizes come from the regulation's annex on horizontal signs (załącznik nr 2 to the 2003 Rozporządzenie on road signs and signals; consolidated text [Dz.U. 2019 poz. 2311](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU20190002311), checked against the amendments up to Dz.U. 2026 poz. 132; section numbers below are the annex's).
  - **Lane lines:** P-1b dashed lines (0.12 m, 2 m dashes, 4 m gaps, up to 70 km/h, 2.2.1.2) between lanes going the same way; P-1a (4 m / 8 m, 2.2.1.1) above 70 km/h, which no detail-box road reaches.
  - **Between opposing lanes:** P-4 double solid (two 0.12 m lines 0.12 m apart, 2.2.1.10) where either way has ≥ 2 lanes; the annex requires it on two-way carriageways with three or more lanes. On a one-lane-each-way road, a dashed line above 30 km/h. Nothing at 30 km/h or less, which is local practice for residential streets and not from the annex. Nothing on carriageways narrower than 5.8 m, where 2.2.1 forbids lane lines.
  - **Stop lines and zebras:** P-14 stop lines (0.5 m deep, 0.75 m rectangles 0.25 m apart, 4.2.5), 0.1 m inside the edge end, which with the full `STOP_SETBACK` leaves exactly the 2.0 m the annex asks for between P-14 and the zebra (4.1, 4.2.5). Which approaches get one: every signalised one (`to.signal`; the annex puts P-14 on every lane before signals, 7.6.2.4), also on short links that gave up part or all of the setback (see above), where it stays at the edge end, where traffic stops, 0.25–2.0 m before the zebra and never on it; and unsignalised approaches of major roads (`axis` `'ave'`) that kept the full setback and have a zebra. The annex requires P-14 before pedestrian crossings at junctions only "na wlotach drogi z pierwszeństwem" (on priority-road approaches) and recommends it on subordinate and equal-priority ones (4.2.1); it has no lane-count rule, and the geodata has no D-1/A-7 signs, so the major road stands in for the priority road. Every other approach gets none: drivers stop at the zebra's near edge (4.1), and P-12/P-13 need a B-20 STOP or A-7 give-way sign (4.2.3, 4.2.4), which the geodata does not have. P-10 zebras are 4.0 m deep in 0.5 m stripes (4.2.1), next to the junction box, and only where pedestrians cross: road-paint.js paints exactly `pedestrian-routes.js`'s `crosswalks` (traffic.js hands them over as `trafficSystem.crosswalks`; see `docs/actors.md` for which arms get one), so an unsignalised major-road approach with no zebra gets no P-14 either.
  - **No bike lanes** (removed 2026-10-28: the red paint looked poor). The player's cyclist rides in the roadway.
  - **Measured on the furnished network:** 2771 P-1b pieces (100.4 km). 426 stop-line pieces of 2684 approaches: 377 signalised (213 of them squeezed, closer than 2 m to the zebra) and 49 unsignalised on major roads before a zebra (117 when they did not need one; 1834 when every approach with the full setback had one, 2658 when they stood 0.25 m before the zebra on every approach, 360 when only signalised approaches had them; requiring two arriving lanes instead of a major road would have given 92); 850 approaches are on short links without the setback. About 1.8 km of P-4 line, because most multi-lane roads are divided, so their centre is a median. 1602 zebras over the 6.1 × 3.7 km box (3310 over the 8.2 × 5.7 km one, 22 of them split; the other counts here are the smaller box's) (1385 before the ports above, when many arms' curbs lay out of reach), none overlapping another and none over another road's lanes but 2 (where one arm's two roadways diverge); every one ends on a curb island, except the 14 of split crossings, which end at a median or tram strip that is no island, with the zebra over the other roadway across it (`docs/actors.md`). Before split crossings there were 1375 and none in the Dworzec complex. Painted at every end of every paint edge, as before, there were 3616: 276 overlapping pairs (X shapes and doubled zebras where the short links of a junction complex, a divided road's or a tram corridor's corners, each painted their own), 2208 with an end in the roadway and 234 over another road's lanes, including on streets outside the strongly connected set where nobody walks.
  - **Not painted:** no "BUS" lane text (P-22), because there is no bus-lane data.

## Street furniture (`street-details.js`)
Built before the road network, so everything that stands near a lane is checked against `roadClearance(x, z)` (city-config): the distance outside the nearest roadway as road-network.js lays its runs out (`LANE_GUIDE`). Near merged junctions those runs stray up to ~2 m from the real centrelines `nearestRoads` measures, so a curb-outline test alone left poles in lanes. `clearOfRoads(x, z, r)` asks for `r + 0.3` m. `LANE_GUIDE` holds, per link:
  - the link from merged junction centre to junction centre through its real bends, at its own half width, or wider where the lanes' `LANE_CLEAR` reaches past the curbs (at least 1.2 m on a one-way, a quarter of the width + 1.2 m on a two-way: the 3.3 m streets);
  - the chords road-network.js cuts near junctions, where it places no shape node (`JUNCTION_END` 25 m): from each junction centre to the points 15 and 25 m along, and the whole link under 50 m;
  - for a one-way link with an opposite one-way link of the same name nearby, a 1.2 m lane strip 1.8 m right of it: step 3 used to put every such pair onto one centreline with each way's lanes from 1.8 m right (now only pairs drawn under 4 m apart; the strip stays as a superset).
  Links with a tram strip beside them (flags bits 3/4) are moved 3.25 m to the tracks' side, as road-network.js lays their roadway out.
  Without the last three, 18 LED poles, 6 bollards and 2 parking machines cost 2.3, 1.1 and 0.9 km: the lanes they stood in lay up to 2 m outside the old guide.
- **Crosswalk landings.** `JUNCTIONS` (city-config: every merged junction with its centre and arms) gives each arm's crosswalk: the `CROSSWALK` band `STOP_SETBACK` inside a stop line estimated as in `stopBack` from the arms crossing it (both imported from road-network.js; the estimate does not squeeze short links), from curb island to curb island: like pedestrian-routes.js, each side runs on past the arm's curb line to the first `blockAt` within `CROSSWALK_REACH`. The estimate is a superset: it keeps furniture off arms that end up with no crosswalk (see `docs/actors.md`). `clearOfCrosswalks(x, z, r)` keeps signal poles, lamps and the curbside furniture off the whole crosswalk depth up to 2.6 m behind the curb, since `pedestrian-routes.js` drops a crosswalk with any footprint on its 2 m landing (±0.4 m). Bends (two arms about in line) have none. On the furnished network, 1887 of the 1928 crosswalk tracks the bare walkable allows survive (1852 of 1886 before zebras had to end on a curb island and stopped overlapping; 1851 of 1884 before the stop-line setback; 1789 before the landings were kept clear, when signal poles closed 41, bollards 8, parking machines 1); the 41 left are furniture on a landing (of the 33 before, where the estimate missed the network's crosswalk: 19 signal poles, 8 LED poles; not broken down again). Placing the crosswalks from the stop line with the full setback even on squeezed links lost 52 of the bare 1884, pushed into the junction box; giving up the setback first brought them back.
- **Signals (section 1).** Per junction (sites sharing a `junction` are built once), the arms of one road (within ~35° of one line: both ways, or a divided road's two roadways) form a signal group, ranked like `sidesAndGroups` (major first, then width), so group 0 should match the network's. On each arm, the corner is where its curb lines clear the crossing roads' curbs; 4.8–15.6 m past it (`CROSSWALK` + 0.5 m on: first between the crosswalk and the stop line `STOP_SETBACK` behind it, as Polish signals stand, then level with the line and back from it), 0.75, 1.0 or 1.3 m behind the curb and 0.8 m outside the nearest roadway, off the crosswalk landings, one galvanised 4.2 m pole on the right-hand curb (or a median ≥ 1.8 m wide) carries a black S-1 head (200 mm lamps, visors; centre at 3.55 m) facing the arriving traffic, and one on the left-hand curb carries a repeater on arms ≥ 12 m or 3+ lanes. Major arms get a backplate with a yellow border. Every pole also carries a pedestrian head (red standing over green walking figure, a canvas mask) at 2.45 m facing across the arm; it walks with the other group (`traffic.js` `walkOn`), steady green for `walk` s, flashing until that group's amber, red otherwise. Lens and panel geometry carry their lamp in `uv.y` (1 red, 0.5 amber, 0 green; the panel's upper half red), not in world height. No S-2 arrows, no countdowns. `audioSignals` has one entry per pedestrian head, `isAvenue` = it walks with group 0. Poles used to stand 1.2–4.8 m past the corner, inside the stop-line setback: road-network.js runs its turning curves (`pathClear`, 0.8 m clear) from stop line to stop line, cutting the corners, and at 58 corners they passed the pole, which cost 4.2 km of the strongly connected set although every pole was clear of the straight runs; moved back they cost nothing. Measured: 199 junctions, 892 poles (981 before), 475 S-1 heads, 892 pedestrian heads.
- **Name plates (1B).** For each named arm and side, a ray from the curb 3.5 m past the corner across the sidewalk finds the first wall (a `buildingColliders` box, AABB or `obb`, at least 5 m tall) within 14 m that faces the street; a plate goes on it at 3.3 m, one per name within 25 m. Plates follow Poznań's System Informacji Miejskiej (2017 manual, pp. 41–51; ZDM "Nowe tablice ulicowe"): graphite RAL 7043 (#3b3d3f) with white left-set type, the kind as a full lowercase word on a small top line with a patron's given names and titles (`plateLabel(name, district)` → `{ kind, name, district }`: "ulica Jana Henryka" over "Dąbrowskiego", "aleje Karola" over "Marcinkowskiego", "rondo" over "Kaponiera"; "Stary Rynek" and Zaułek/Pasaż/Wzgórze names have no top word; the HUD plate in `main.js` reuses it), the name large below, and a blue RAL 5005 (#1f5bb5) strip along the bottom with the block's district in capitals. 0.315 × 0.22 m in the Old Town and on streets under 10 m, 0.67 × 0.35 m elsewhere (`PLATE_SIZES`), each size its own canvas atlas with a per-instance `plateCell`. No house-number ranges. Headless (tests) there are no colliders and no plates; with the lot rects standing in for walls, 122. The old pole blades are gone.
- **Streetlights (2).** Every curb edge takes the class of the road in front of it (`nearestRoads`): main (≥ 12.5 m, 3+ lanes or a trunk road) 10 m LED bent masts every ~34 m, side streets 7 m ones (the same mesh at 0.7) every ~30 m, both 1.08 m inside the curb with the luminaire over the road and a pooled spot. In the Old Town (Stare Miasto inside buildings.js `OLD_TOWN_BOX`) and within 650 m of the Katedra, non-main edges get cast-iron gas lanterns (ladder bar, four-sided glass) every ~22 m with pooled point lights. Measured: 1109 main, 5941 side, 1227 gas.
- **Curbside (3).** Tall red hydrants with a white band (near one end of about half the edges ≥ 30 m), Poczta Polska post boxes (8% of edges), parking machines on non-main edges in Stare Miasto, Jeżyce, Łazarz and Wilda, and bollards 7–12 m from both ends of Old Town and Stare Miasto side-street edges (clear of the crosswalk landings). All within 1 m of the curb. Measured: 1869, 261, 489, 2469.
- **Wide sidewalks (3B).** Press kiosks, flower stands and advertising columns (słupy ogłoszeniowe) 3.8 m or more behind the curb, only where the footprint stays on the block and off its lot rects (`inLot`, frames turned into place), 320 m (columns) or 420 m (kiosks) apart. Measured: 76 kiosks, 67 flower stands, 120 columns. No bike-share docks (Poznański Rower Miejski closed in 2022).
- **Cost to the network:** 14,520 footprints leave 4294 edges / 193.5 km of the bare 193.8 km (99.8%; 184.6 km before the guide and pole fixes above). Leaving out one kind at a time, only hydrants gain anything (0.2 km).
- **Player colliders:** every footprint's prop is a static collider in `colliders` (main.js appends them to `allColliders` after the network is built): an upright box about the visible shaft or cabinet (signal poles 0.16 m to 4.3 m, LED masts 0.28 m up the shaft, gas lanterns, hydrants, post boxes, parking machines, bollards 0.14 m, columns) and oriented boxes (`obb`) for kiosks and flower stands. 14,196 in all, none within 2 m of a track (`clearOfRails`); controllers.js buckets them into its 48 m cells with the buildings, so a lookup only sees the few along its own curb. Trees have none.

## Tram infrastructure
`tram-infra.js` builds everything static about the trams from `GEO.rail` (`kind: 'tram'`) and `GEO.tramStops`, and the mainline railway from the `kind: 'rail'` tracks (see **Mainline railway** below), after the road network. The placements are the pure `layoutTramInfrastructure(network)` and `layoutRailInfrastructure(network)` (no three.js), which `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` runs on the real network.
- **Rails:** each track is cut into straight pieces of up to 4 m (`PIECE`), so it follows bends and curbs. Each piece gets two instanced rail heads, 70 mm wide at 1435 mm gauge, and a 42 mm dark groove inside each head. On street-running track the heads are flush: top at y 0.016 and the grooves at 0.014, both above the road paint (0.012). That keeps them from z-fighting and puts them over the markings. Pieces over a curb island sit on its 0.22 m top. Other off-street track lies on a ballast box (top 0.14) with raised heads (0.27). **The roadway wins over the trackbed** (`layoutTrackBeds`, its `beds` in the layout): the box is 2.9 m (`BED_HALF` 1.45 m a side) where it fits, narrowed down to 2.4 m (`BED_MIN`) to keep 0.05 m off the roadway, and otherwise left out with the heads flush (level crossings, and `street` 0 track that runs down a carriageway). The roadway here is every OSM carriageway (the link's width along its own points, less a tram strip beside it, flags bits 3/4; a tram link with no side bit has its tracks on the carriageway) plus 1.8 m either side of every lane centreline and turning path of the network, sampled every 0.5 m along the piece. The sleepers scale with a narrowed box (0.98 m half length at 2.4 m against 1.19 m). Before (2026-10-28), a fixed 2.9 m box went wherever no lane centreline came within 3.5 m of a piece's middle, which left 297 m² of ballast on carriageways and 7 m² on lanes (25 pieces centred on a carriageway): most on 28 Czerwca 1956 roku 55–80 m south of Hetmańska (84 m², where its one-lane carriageway's lanes keep off part of it), Drobna, Głogowska by Hetmańska and at the station, Słowiańska, Mostowa, Lewandowskiej, Winogrady, Most Teatralny and Roosevelta; now none (139 of 7899 pieces flush, 61 narrowed, 31.0 → 30.5 km of ballast). Hetmańska's own tracks are street-running (`street` 1, flush) and had no ballast on them. `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` samples every bed against the carriageways and lanes. The ballast shader draws concrete sleepers every 0.6 m from a per-instance `tie` attribute (piece length, arc-length phase), fading to their mean colour when they get small on screen. Pieces over water with no deck are dropped.
- **Overhead line:** a contact wire at `WIRE_Y` 5.8 m runs along every track polyline. Supports stand every ~30 m (`STATION`) along each track, unless one already carries that track within 12 m. From the track, `march` looks out on both sides for the first clear spot: on land, ≥ 2 m from every lane centreline and turning path (`laneDistance` also files the network's connector curves: on lanes alone, poles stood on 15 turns and cost 27 edges) and ≥ 1.75 m from every track. It prefers a curb, with the pole 0.6 m behind the curb edge. If no curb is found, it takes the first clear spot, which can be a centre pole between two tracks or a pole on a reservation. Where both sides are found, a street track gets two 7.6 m poles with a span wire at 6.75 m. Within 900 m of the Ratusz, streets with under 15 m between the two spots get wall rosettes 3.8 m behind the curbs instead (an approximation of the house fronts). Otherwise the nearest side within 9 m gets a pole with a cantilever arm and a stay. Off-street track only looks 5 m out. Droppers hang wherever a span or arm crosses a track. Poles join the player colliders.
- **Stops:** each stop node snaps to the nearest tram track within 25 m. Nodes within 20 m on one track merge. The platform goes on the right of the travel direction: on one-way track, the right of its points; on two-way track, the side away from a parallel track 2–8 m off (both sides are tried if there is none).
  - **Island:** a platform 1.35 m from the track centreline, 33 m long (shorter on short chains, ≥ 18 m), 2.6, 2.0 or 1.5 m wide, 0.25 m high, with a yellow tactile strip. It is curved along the track as 3 m pieces on the curb material. Every sample must be on land, ≥ 1.75 m from every lane centreline (`LANE_KEEP`; road-network's box test reaches that far on diagonal streets) and clear of other tracks.
  - **Curb stop:** if no island fits, the stop stands on a sidewalk within 12 m of the track. Passengers cross the lane to board, as at the real Poznań stops that have no island.
  - **Furniture:** a glass shelter (with a back-lit advert panel that lights by `uInteriorGlow`) when 2 m or more of depth is free, a stop sign with a name plate, and a ticket machine. The name plates come from one canvas atlas (a yellow "TRAMWAJ" band over the Polish name on ZTM green; no logos), indexed by a per-instance `plateCell`. Shelters, signs and machines join the player colliders. Platforms do not, so the player can drive and walk across them.
  - The count: 129 stops (102 islands) from 142 nodes. The rest have no track within 25 m (a Pl. Wielkopolski node on a disused siding, a Stare Zoo node in the old depot yard: closed track no route uses, so `GEO.rail` leaves it out) or no room. The Ostrów Tumski stops (Katedra, Rondo Śródka) and Fredry are on track that OSM tags closed for rebuilding; the build keeps it through the route relations ([`geodata.md`](geodata.md#poznań-snapshots-toolsdatapoz_json)).
- **Obstacle rule:** nothing goes to the road network. The test feeds the layout's `footprints` to `buildRoadNetwork` as obstacles and checks that the network stays almost whole. Measured: 4294 → 4282 edges, 193.5 → 193.1 km with the furniture (4287 / 193.3 km before the stop-line setback moved the turning curves' ends back). If you register any of it as an obstacle, re-measure.
- **Mainline railway:** the 385 `rail` tracks (93 km) are cut into straight pieces of up to 8 m (`RAIL_PIECE`; a 300 m curve is 27 mm off the chord). The world is flat and Poznań's railway crosses streets and tram lines on bridges and in underpasses, so there are no level crossings: a piece is dropped when its centreline (sampled at its ends, quarters and middle) comes within 3.5 m of a roadway (`RAIL_ROAD_KEEP`, the 2.5 m half bed + 1 m; `roadClearance` over the `LANE_GUIDE` carriageways, junction boxes included, and the network's lane centrelines less 1.75 m; under a `BRIDGES` deck counts as on the road) or within 5 m of a tram track centreline (`RAIL_TRAM_KEEP`: half bed + 1 m + the tram's half ballast). Pieces over open water (rail bridges have no deck) or outside the detail box go too. Measured: 8453 pieces kept (66 km, 1540 in the Poznań Główny yard); dropped 3009 outside the box, 29 over water, 282 at roads, 43 at tram tracks. Wherever a kept piece meets a dropped one inside the box, a 0.5 m concrete end wall 7.5 m wide and 1.1 m tall (`RAIL_END_H`; in `RailEndWalls`, an oriented player collider) stands across the bed and out to the corridor boundary on both sides, just into the gap, so the cut reads as a bridge abutment or the edge of an underpass: 143 walls.
  - **Corridor boundary:** along the outer edge of each track bundle, 3.6 m from the outer track's centreline (`RAIL_FENCE_O`, 1.1 m past the bed's foot), a concrete plinth carries a 2 m welded-mesh fence (`RailFence`: a plane per segment, PKP grey-green, its `maskNode` drawing posts every 2.5 m, top and bottom rails and a 5 × 20 cm mesh that turns into a 25% 2×2 screen-door once a wire is under a pixel; per-instance `fence` = segment length and arc length along the run, so the posts keep their rhythm). A piece's side is an outer edge when no track centre lies within 5.5 m of a point 3 m beyond the fence (at its middle and ends); each of its five samples must be on land, ≥ 1 m outside every roadway and off road decks (`RAIL_FENCE_ROAD`), ≥ 3 m from every tram track and ≥ 3 m from every rail track centreline (off every bed). Vertices sit at the piece joints on the mitred normal, so consecutive pieces form one run with no gaps or overlaps at bends; run ends within 2 m of each other (across a switch, into another track chain) are joined at their midpoint. Runs stop at cuts against the end walls. A run end within 8 m (`RAIL_STUB`) of another run's end or an end wall's corner gets a straight stub to it when the stub keeps the same clearances (46 stubs). Where a road, a block, a plaza or open land with no track within 6 m lies 3–12 m beyond (the street alongside, the station car parks, which are unmapped ground), the plinth is a 1.2 m wall (`RAIL_WALL_H`) under the fence; between parallel bundles and along the water it is 0.5 m (`RAIL_PLINTH_H`). Plinths and walls go in `RailEndWalls`. Each segment is an oriented player collider up to the fence top. Masts keep 0.4 m off the boundary. Measured: 266 runs, 3762 segments, 29.6 km (25.9 km over a wall; 5.6 km in the Poznań Główny box, 2.7 km of it walled).
  - **Player keep-out:** the ground modes may not enter the corridor at all, fence or no fence. `layoutRailInfrastructure` rasterises it at 1 m in 64 m tiles kept only where there is corridor (`corridor.has(x, z)`, allocation-free; `buildTramInfrastructure` returns it as `railCorridor`, which main.js hands to `ThirdPersonControllerManager` as `keepOut`). It is each kept piece's rectangle 3.4 m either side (`RAIL_KEEPOUT`, just inside the 3.6 m boundary, so where there is a fence its collider stops a body first), 0.25 m past both ends (under the end walls), plus, in 2 m steps, the strip out to a neighbouring track on any side that fails the boundary's outer-edge test (so the strips between bundles too close to fence are closed and nothing reaches past a fence), unless the way across comes within 3 m of a roadway or 2.5 m of a tram track. Since kept pieces stay 3.5 m from every roadway, the roads crossing the cuts stay open. Measured: 344k cells; no roadway point beyond the 143 cut ends is inside; 2 of 3762 points 0.8 m outside the fence are (a fence 3.1 m from a neighbouring track, a stub). See `docs/architecture.md` for the controllers' side. Each piece lies on a trapezoid granite bed (`RailBallast`: 5 m at the foot, 4 m on top at 0.30 m, plus a few mm per track so overlapping beds at switches don't z-fight) whose shader (the tram ballast's, parameterised) draws 2.6 m concrete sleepers every 0.6 m; at 4.5 m track centres the beds close up into one yard surface. Two 60E1 heads (0.16 m) stand on top. Every track is taken as electrified (PKP 3 kV DC; no `electrified` tag in the geodata): a contact wire at 5.6 m and a messenger at 7.0 m run as straight chords over up to three consecutive kept pieces, never across a cut. Each cut end gets a support 1.5 m back (else 4 or 8 m); then supports every ~55 m (`RAIL_STATION`) along each track unless one already carries it within 22 m: a mast (0.3 m box section, 8.2 m) on the first clear spot within 7 m of the track with a sloping cantilever tube and a registration arm; failing that (inside a yard), a portal: two 9.6 m masts on the first clear spots up to 40 m out on both sides and a 0.5 m beam at 8.9 m with droppers to every track it crosses. A clear spot is on land, ≥ 2 m outside every roadway (`RAIL_MAST_ROAD`), ≥ 3 m from every tram track (`RAIL_MAST_TRAM`), ≥ 2.6 m from every rail track, and on a block only within 3.5 m of its curb; neither an arm nor a portal beam may reach over a roadway or a tram track. Measured: 1007 masts, 827 cantilevers, 113 portals; every piece has a support within 45 m, and 1 of the 143 cut ends none within 10 m. Masts join the player colliders; their footprints and the boundary's (sampled every 2 m) leave the network as the tram ones do (4282 edges / 193.1 km). Rail heads, wires, masts and arms go into the tram meshes (`TramRails`, `CatenaryWires`, `CatenaryArms`), so the railway adds three instanced meshes (`RailBallast`, `RailEndWalls`, `RailFence`). The layout takes ~650–750 ms headless (lane, road and track lookups dominate). `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` checks the clearances, the end walls at every cut, the masts, the boundary (off roads, tram tracks and beds; fenced along the yard) and the keep-out (the beds in, the roads at the cuts and the ground outside the fence out); `tests/controllers.test.cjs` the controllers' keep-out. Not drawn: platforms, station roofs, signals, points (switch blades), the railway land itself (the `landuse=railway` rings are not exported; beds only cover the tracks), embankments, bridges and viaducts (the cuts stand in for them).
- **Public surface:** `stops` (`{ name, x, z, heading, railIndex, s, dir, side, kind }`). `x, z` is the stop point on the track. `s` is the arc length along `GEO.rail[railIndex].pts`. `dir` is +1 when trams serving it run along the points. `heading` is their travel heading (0 = +z). `kind` is `'island'` or `'curb'`. The tram actors can dwell there.

## Buildings and collision
- `buildCityBlocks` walks the lot rects of every `BLOCKS` entry except parks (`park > 0.6`) and medians. For each rect it subtracts `LANDMARK_RESERVE`, splits deep lots (`splitDeep`), and calls `populateBlock(minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ, …, district)` on each piece. A building is a set of non-overlapping box parts `{x0, x1, z0, z1, y0, y1, roof?}`, and `commit()` emits them.
  - **Frontages and corners** come from the block's curb outline (`BLOCKS[i].poly`), not from the rect's position: `frontages()` marks a rect side as a street front (`blk.front`) when 3 of its 5 samples lie within `FRONT_REACH` (10 m) of the outline (lot rects stand 4.6 m in from the curb), or when 3 of them look along the side's outward normal at the curb within `FRONT_RAY` (18 m) with no lot rect of the block (`srcRects`) in the way (the frontage frames leave slivers of up to ~13 m uncovered behind the curb; without the ray 31% of the lot area was frontless courtyard, with it 19%), and a corner (`blk.corner`) as `'street'` where two fronts meet and the rect corner lies near the outline, `'notch'` where it doesn't. `residentialBlock` puts a corner house on each street corner, rows (`ROW_KINDS`: `kamienica`, `rynekHouse`, `villa`, `modernist1930s`, `postwarBlock`, `infill`, `low`, `gap`) along each front between them, and builds up the core the rows leave (more than `ROW_REACH` 36 m from a front, kept `INNER_YARD` 6 m off) like a rect with no front: `innerLot` / `innerBuildings` put the profile's `inner` kinds there (`INNER_RES`: rear houses across the lot with a light court between rows, a workshop hall, a free-standing school or office pavilion, or a yard with the odd shed; Ostrów's and the villas' profiles keep mostly yards). The Targi block (the curb island under the Iglica, `FAIR_BLOCK`) takes `FAIRGROUND`: exhibition halls 11–18 m tall in 12–18 m alleys behind 8 m forecourts. A lot piece touching a park ring in a part-park block (park share ≤ 0.6) is cut into ~40 m cells (`PARK_CELL`) and only the cells clear of every park are built; before, 0.42 km² of buildings stood in parks. `projectsBlock` adds the estate's services in the bands between slab rows and past the last one (`SERVICES`: a 3-storey school, a 1-2-storey shop pavilion or lock-up garages, 6 m off the slabs). Each lot's profile then goes through `steerProfile`, toward its block's real density (`BLOCKS[i].built` / `.levels` from `GEO.blockStats`, OSM footprints). `KIND_SHARE` is the share each profile builds unsteered. Below it, kamienica profiles get war gaps on that share of the frontage, bare yards and no core buildings under 0.7, and the other profiles skip lots. Above it, the gaps and yards go, and from 1.3× (`dense`) oficyny ring every courtyard on 3 m yards. Median storeys set the kamienica and corner floors. Panel estates whose block is lower than 7 storeys or denser than 1.4× take the kamienica profile. Part-park blocks build 20 m cells (`PARK_CELL`); a cell is park when its centre or two corners are. city-grid.js gives their parks a lawn (rings at least 80% inside the block) and trees 10 m inside the ring. Big plant rooms are capped at `HVAC_MAX` 12 m. Measured over the detail box (headless raster against OSM): 16.2% of the land built against 17.3% real; Łazarz 31.5 / 29.7, Jeżyce 31.0 / 29.0, Rataje 14.7 / 15.9, Główna 19.9 / 16.8 (it was 41%), Ostrów-Śródka 9.4 / 8.3 (it was 21%), Św. Marcin's side streets 29.1 / 37.8.
  - **Profiles** (`blockProfile`): offices within 240 m of Bałtyk, Andersia/AND2/PFC and Collegium Altum; `CESARSKA` in a box round Zamek Cesarski, Collegium Minus, Teatr Wielki and the 1956 crosses (+160 m); `RYNEK` for rects with a front facing the `Stary Rynek` plaza (`blk.rynekSides`: probes 11 m out land inside its outline); then by osiedle (Stare Miasto splits into Old Town/Garbary/Chwaliszewo and Centrum at `OLD_TOWN_BOX`, Ostrów Tumski/Śródka within 650 m of the Katedra). The osiedla the 8.2 × 5.7 km box took in (Phase 13.2): Nowe Winogrady, Żegrze and Piątkowo are `ESTATE` (panel slabs that `steerProfile` does not turn into kamienice; a strip lot too narrow for a slab row gets one slab along it); Stary Grunwald, Grunwald Północ and Górczyn, the garden suburbs (Ogrody, Grunwald Południe, Naramowice, ...) and the industrial east and south (Antoninek-Zieliniec-Kobylepole, Starołęka) pick their kind per block from its real median storeys (`GEO.blockStats`: slabs from 7, villas up to 3). Measured built share, generated / real: Nowe Winogrady Południe 14.9 / 17.2 %, Żegrze 13.2 / 12.8 %, Górczyn 27.5 / 21.5 %, Ogrody 10.2 / 15.7 %, Grunwald Południe 6.8 / 10.0 %; with the default kamienica profile Nowe Winogrady Południe and Żegrze came out 27.5 and 25.5 % built, Grunwald Południe 19.6 %. Facade materials are named in `F` (four plaster colours, red and yellow brick, grey sandstone 4.4 m storeys, four Rynek pastels, three glass, and five `createPanelBlockMaterial` colours at 2.8 m storeys (`PANELS`: grey, cream and three repainted ones with accent bands) for the osiedle slabs and `postwarBlock`). Buildings are massed in whole storeys of their facade's `floorHeight` (the `storeys` list reads it from the options), so a new facade must carry one.
  - **Pitched roofs** are specs on the parts they cover, `part.roof = { shape: 'gable'|'hip'|'mansard', mat: 'tile'|'slate'|'copper', ridge: 'x'|'z' (lot-frame axis), rise }` (set by `pitch()`); the box and its collider stop at the eave (`y1`). Chimney stacks read `rise` so they clear the ridge. `commit()` turns each spec into a roofs.js roof (ridge `'z'` = `ry` π/2, since roofs.js runs a gable's ridge along `w`) unless parts stacked on the part cover over half of it (`roofCover`). The eaves overhang by `EAVE` 0.3 m across the ridge (along it too for hips, mansards and free-standing houses, never at a gable end on a party wall), the cross-section scaled so the pitch and ridge height stay.
- Irregular blocks carry frontage frames (`BLOCKS[i].frames`, from the geodata build). The generator lays the lots out in a frame turned by θ about p (`xf`), as if on a grid block. `placeInFrame` then turns everything the block emitted into place. **Any new per-block detail list must be added to the list of lists in `placeInFrame`**, or it will be misplaced on turned blocks. Pitched roofs are the exception: they carry their own yaw, so `populateBlock` takes `roofs.mark()` before the layout runs and calls `roofs.transform(mark, toWorld, xf.th)` next to `placeInFrame`.
- **Colliders** are `{ minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ, minY?, maxY, obb? }`, where a missing `minY` means 0. `obb: { cx, cz, hx, hz, cs, sn }` marks an oriented box, and min/max are then its world AABB. `pushCollider` emits exactly one collider per building part, from `y0` to `y1`.
  - Commit 49b2ef2 removed the old whole-block collider up to 26 m. It covered yards, courts and gaps, so players hit invisible walls. Make colliders follow the visible geometry, and never add coarse block-sized ones.
  - `minY` lets the player pass under raised parts.
  - main.js drops oriented colliders and those with `minY >= 3.5` from the traffic obstacles. Oriented boxes would over-block through their AABB.
  - The narrow phase is `buildingContact` in controllers.js: a circle for walkers, cyclists and the helicopter, SAT for the car. `tests/controllers.test.cjs` covers the corner cases.
- **Roofs** (`roofs.js`, `createRoofs({ materials: { tile, slate, copper, window, gable }, max, parent })`): one static instance family for pitched roofs (gable, hip, mansard, gambrel), one `InstancedMesh` per unit piece × material (4 × 3), plus the dormers (`Roof-dormer-tile`, `Roof-dormer-slate` and `Roof-dormerWindow`) and the Rynek gable screens (`Roof-gable-stepped`, `Roof-gable-volute`), always visible under `CityBlocks`. A gable or gambrel is one stretched prism; a hip or mansard is a prism over the middle `w − d` of the ridge plus a `d/2 × d` cap at each end, so all four slopes share one pitch on any plan. `add(shape, mat, x, y, z, w, d, h, ry)` only stages a record (false when the pool of `MAX_ROOF_INSTANCES` 64k is full). For a turned frame take `mark()` before populating and call `transform(mark, toWorld, th)` after it (buildings.js `populateBlock`). `finish()` runs once (buildings.js calls it after the facade boxes), before scene culling: it writes the matrices into an `instanceMatrix` exactly `count` long, because culling skips meshes with spare capacity, and hides meshes left empty. Sloped normals rely on three's instance normal transform handling non-uniform scale. `tests/roofs.test.cjs` checks normals, pitch and counts.
  - **Dormers** (`addDormers(shape, mat, …same arguments as add…, { rand, facing })`): `planDormers` puts a row of 1–3 gabled dormers (11 triangles, its own roof rising `DORMER_ROOF` 0.45 of its 1.0–1.7 m front wall; 1.2–1.7 m wide, one per 4.4–6 m) on each slope, the front wall 0.8 m in from the eave (0.5 m, low on the steep band, on a mansard), reaching back until the slope meets its ridge (at most `DORMER_DEPTH` 3.6 m), 0.35 m under the main ridge, off the gable ends (1 m) or inside a hip's end slopes. A roof with a rise under 2.2 m, a ridge under 5 m or a half-depth under 3 m gets none, and so do copper roofs. `facing(dx, dz)` is the probability of a slope by its outward direction. Each dormer is two records: the body in the roof material (front and cheeks render as gable-end render) and a pane in `createDormerWindowMaterial` (casement frame, mullion and transom; 38% lit at night by an instance-index hash). buildings.js `commit()` adds them for `o.dormers` (`DORMER_P` 0.45 of kamienice and non-Rynek corner houses, decided off the building's `tone`), every street slope and 25% of courtyard slopes, from `mulberry32` seeded by the tone and the part, so the block's own draws are untouched. Measured over the 8.2 × 5.7 km box (headless, 2026-10-28): 18,048 dormers (10,930 tile, 7,118 slate), ≈ 235k triangles, 3 meshes before chunking; the roof records total 82.6k of the 128k pool (46.5k roofs, 36.1k dormer records). The former 64k pool ran full over the larger box; the 6.1 × 3.7 km box used 46.8k (10,472 dormers).
  - **Rynek gable screens** (`addGable(style, x, y, z, w, rise, ry, tint)`): the attic wall a Stary Rynek house raises in its facade plane in front of its roof's gable end, 0.45 m thick and 0.2 m proud of the wall, as wide as the wall and `rise / GABLE_PEAK` tall, so the skyline clears the roof everywhere (`tests/roofs.test.cjs`). `'stepped'`: four 0.2-high steps a side and a top block (peak 0.8, 80 triangles). `'volute'`: a pediment over two tiers whose flanks are ogee scrolls curling at the foot, obelisk finials on the corners (peak 0.72, 304 triangles). Both outlines have ledges at 0.4 and 0.8 of the height, where `createGableScreenMaterial` (+1 pipeline) draws white string courses; it also draws the coping, a plinth band, three attic windows and an oculus, and tints the render with the per-instance `gableTint` (the house's facade `stoneColor`, linear). buildings.js sets `roof.front` on the gables turned to the square (`rynekHouse`, and the Rynek corner houses' wings on a Rynek side); `commit()` adds a screen there when the part's wall is on the lot's front, the style from `gableStyle(tone)` (25% keep the plain render triangle, 35% stepped, 40% volute), so the choice is per house and draws nothing from the block's generator. Measured: 21 stepped and 21 volute screens, 8.1k triangles, 2 draw calls.
- `LANDMARK_RESERVE` (buildings.js) lists the `LANDMARK_SITES` names (Ratusz, Fara, Zamek Cesarski, Bałtyk, AND2, …) whose outline, plus a 3 m margin, stays free of generic buildings. Bridge decks are kept clear the same way.
- **Śródka's mural square** ("Opowieść Śródecka", 2015): `SRODKA_MURAL` in city-config.js holds the east wall of ul. Śródka 3 (OSM way 238466003's corners, 20.25 m, at about 52.4113 N 16.9535 E; an earlier estimate of 52.4123 N 16.9573 E falls on Podwale, 270 m off) and the house frame (`toWorld(lx, lz)`, `ang`: local z out of the wall, over the square). landmarks-heritage.js builds the 13 m deep, four-storey house (14.4 m eave, hipped tile roof; world-grid plaster facade, since it is turned), the mural as one plane 3 cm in front of the wall, and the square: granite setts on the deck out to 1 m short of the curb, three benches facing the wall, two hedged planters and a lamp (7 `obb` colliders). The mural is a 2048 × 1384 canvas painted once at build time (`paintMural`, seeded): four pastel kamienice with stepped, volute and plain gables against a painted sky, shuttered windows with flower boxes and neighbours, a gateway opening on a lane that runs off to a church tower, shop fronts, a black cat on a sill and pigeons on a cornice. Without a DOM the wall is plain render. +2 draw calls (the mural and the hedge material), about 300 triangles. buildings.js adds the house and the square (out to `square` 17 m) to `LANDMARK_RESERVE` as an outline. `tests/srodka-mural.test.cjs` checks the wall's frame and that the house and square stand on the island, off the roadways.

## Near/far detail levels
- **Building detail streaming:** cornices, brick chimney stacks (on the eave line), skylights (flat roofs), HVAC (modern roofs only), balcony stacks, awnings, storefront signs and renovation scaffolds (green netting, or one of `WRAP_THEMES` building-wrap adverts) sit per block in a `Quadtree`. `update()` re-queries blocks within `STREAM_RADIUS` (560 m) once the focus has moved 18 m or more. It sorts them nearest first, so the nearest blocks win when a pool's fixed cap (`MAX_*`) runs out, and refills the instanced pools.
  - Uploads are limited to the used range (`flagRange` → `addUpdateRange`; commit b574893).
  - The pools are `frustumCulled = false`, which also keeps them out of scene-culling.
- **Balconies** (`createBalconies(group)` → `{ begin, add(b, focusX, focusZ), end }`, driven from the buildings `update`; the records live in `blk.balconies`):
  - A record is a stack of kamienica balconies, one per storey and no stairs: a 1.0 m deep stone slab (2.4 m wide, or the record's `w` clamped to 1.5–3 m), black wrought-iron railings on three sides and two curved console brackets.
  - Within `NEAR_DISTANCE` 90 m of the focus, each balcony is an instance of real geometry, up to `MAX_NEAR_BALCONIES` (256) in total. The `maskNode` cuts balusters, rails, the ornament band and the bracket profile; a per-instance `balconyStyle` picks the band (rings, serpentine scroll or diamond lattice), hashed from the stack position unless the record sets `style`.
  - Farther out, or once the near budget is spent, a stack is a single open box whose `maskNode` draws a slab band and a picket railing per storey from `balconySize = (storey, box height, width)`.
  - buildings.js `commit()` emits them for half the kamienice, corner houses and 1930s houses (`BALCONY_P`), on street fronts only: 1–3 stacks per front (one per ~7 m), one slab width (1.8–3 m) and railing style per building, from the first floor (the second over shops, clear of the awnings) up to six storeys. Measured over the 8.2 × 5.7 km box (headless, foci on a 100 m grid, 2026-10-28): 17.4k stacks city-wide (10.1k over the 6.1 × 3.7 km box), at most 1243 within `STREAM_RADIUS` (Jeżyce) against the 2400 cap and 245 near balconies within 90 m (Garbary; 228 on the same grid before) against 256. The other streamed pools peak at cornices 1985 / 7000, chimneys 2055 / 3000, skylights 524 / 1000, HVAC 157 / 1600, awnings 942 / 1800 and scaffolds 43 / 120: the densest spots are in the old centre, so the larger box raised only the skylights (Główna, 524 against 372).
  - The record holds `x, y, z, ry, facing, storey, landings, h` (plus optional `w`, `style`): `z` sits 0.55 m off the facade, `facing` turns local +z into the street, `y` is the lowest slab top, and `h = (landings - 1) * storey + 1`.
- **Trees** (`trees.js`): three species, by `species` index on each site: 0 lime (lipa, the default: most park and wide-street trees), 1 Norway maple (narrow streets, plaza planters, leftover slabs), 2 horse chestnut (a share of parks and wide streets). Callers pick the index (`street-details.js` section 4, `city-grid.js` park sites, `buildings.js` leftover slabs). Each species has one tile of the 2×2 leaf atlas (tile 3 is the far-canopy clump; a 4th species needs a wider atlas and its own `meshIndex` entry) and its own bark in `createTreeMaterial`, keyed off the same index. `tests/trees.test.cjs` paints the atlas on a recording canvas and checks that every path stays in its tile. Trees are procedural rows; real OSM `natural=tree` positions are not in the geodata. `LOD_NEAR` is 105 m, `LOD_MID` 420 m, then crown cards, with 6 m of hysteresis. The meshes are regrouped per 200 m cell / species / variant / level. Instance buffers use static usage on purpose: DynamicDrawUsage re-uploads in every pass of every frame.
- **Scene culling** is automatic for anything under the seven named roots; thresholds and opt-outs are in [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#culling-scene-cullingjs-registering-new-static-geometry).

## Batching
- `batchStaticGroupMeshes(root, excludeMaterials)` merges all non-instanced meshes that share a **material object** into one mesh. It keeps only `position`, `normal` and `uv`, and meshes missing any of those are skipped. The originals are removed, so their names, userData and custom attributes are lost.
  - Pass a material in `excludeMaterials` to keep its meshes separate, for example a material with custom attributes, or one whose mesh another module looks up.
  - city-grid.js batches its group at the end of `buildCityGrid`, and landmarks.js at the end of `buildLandmarks` (the `LandmarksDynamic` root is left out).
- Share material instances wherever possible. Every distinct node material is another WGSL pipeline to precompile behind the loading screen, which is why landmarks share "canonical" facade materials.
- landmarks.js re-emits unrotated `BoxGeometry` parts that use a material from `boxLocalTwins` as instanced facade boxes (`createFacadeBoxes`, tsl-materials.js).

## Recipes
**New kind of street furniture** (in `street-details.js` `buildStreetDetails`):
1. Pick sites with the local helpers: `isOnCurbIsland`, `isOnSidewalk(x, z, pad)`, `inPlaza`, `blockAt` (city-config), `isClearOfPoles`, `clearOfRoads(x, z, radius)`, `clearOfRails(x, z, radius)` (city-grid.js) and `inLot` for the property line. To place props along every real block at any bearing, walk `curbEdges` (curb edges ≥ 24 m fronting open roadway, with inward/outward normals), as the lamps and street trees do.
2. Call `addFootprint(x, z, w, d, kind)` for each prop that should block pedestrians and lanes, and mind the obstacle rule above: check `clearOfRoads`, and keep it within ~1 m of the curb or beyond 3.8 m (the walking lines run 1.7 and 2.8 m in, ±0.4 m, `pedestrian-routes.js`).
3. Build one `InstancedMesh` per geometry and material, and `group.add` it. It is chunk-culled automatically if it passes the thresholds above. Do not set `frustumCulled = false`.
4. For a night light, push a site onto `streetPointSites` (pooled point lights) or `ledLampSites` (pooled spots).
5. For player collision, push a box onto `colliders` (`addCollider(x, z, hx, hz, maxY)`, or an `obb` collider for a turned prop); main.js adds them to `allColliders`. Also keep it off the crosswalks (`clearOfCrosswalks`).
6. For a sound source, follow how `audioSignals` is consumed by `CityAudioSystem`.
7. A new module needs the registration steps in `AGENTS.md`.

**New landmark:**
1. Footprint: add its OSM name to `tools/data/q_poz_landmarks.overpassql` and its curated entry to `tools/poz-extra.mjs`, refetch and rebuild ([`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). It then appears in `LANDMARK_SITES[name]`.
2. The area module (`landmarks-<area>.js`, conventions in the `landmark-kit.js` header): add a block `{ const c0 = colliders.length; …model around a local origin…; group.add(obj); collider(colliders, …); fitToSite([obj], site('Name'), colliders, c0, { height }); }`. `fitToSite` scales the model to the real height, capped by the outline width (+6%), and moves it onto the site centre. It applies the same transform to every collider pushed since `c0`, so author colliders in the model's own coordinates, as axis-aligned boxes. When the site has an `ang`, the model is scaled in the site's own frame and then turned by `ang` about the site centre, `offset` turns with it, and each collider becomes an `obb` collider (the shape buildings.js `pushCollider` emits), which keeps it out of the traffic obstacles. Turned box parts keep the world-space window grid, because the `boxLocalTwins` re-emit skips rotated parts. Reuse the shared facade materials, and use the `boxLocalTwins` keys for box parts.
3. Add the name to `LANDMARK_RESERVE` in buildings.js, or generic buildings will grow through it.
4. Add it to `CITY_LANDMARKS` (city-config.js) for its map pin and the location label; `tests/city-text.test.cjs` fails while a `LANDMARK_SITES` entry has no pin. Optionally add a `TELEPORT_LOCATIONS` arrival.
5. Towers outside the detail box belong in outskirts.js instead (coordinates via `LL()`).

**New streamed building detail:** add a list to `blockDetail` in `populateBlock`, fill it in `commit()`, add it to `placeInFrame`, create a capped pool next to the `MAX_*` constants, and fill it and set its count and `flagRange` in `update()`.

## Determinism
- World building uses no `Math.random`. Only traffic.js and pedestrian-routes.js do.
  - buildings.js reseeds `mulberry32` per block from `(minX, minZ)` at the start of `populateBlock`. A block's buildings therefore depend only on its own bounds and profile: editing one block leaves the others unchanged, but any geodata change to a block's rect reshuffles that block.
  - outskirts.js and trees.js use `mulberry32` with fixed seeds per generator; city-grid.js and street-details.js hash positions (`prand`, `hashPos`); bridges.js uses no randomness.
- Keep new procedural placement on a seeded generator, so screenshots and tests stay reproducible.
- Draw order matters in the shared generators. Inserting `rand()` calls into an existing loop changes everything generated after it in that seed's scope.
