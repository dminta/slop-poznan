# World building at runtime

Frame: x = east, z = south (north = −z), y up, 1 unit = 1 m, origin at the Ratusz; the axes are not turned (`GEO.frame.bearingDeg` 0, true north up; see [`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). Geodata reaches the world builders mostly through `js/city-config.js` exports (`BLOCKS`, `ROADS`, `PLAZAS`, `LAND_RINGS`/`LAND_HOLES`, `WATER`, `OUTSKIRTS`, `BRIDGES`, `LANDMARK_SITES`, `SIGNAL_SITES`). Direct `GEO` consumers also include `city-grid.js` (rail clearance and plaza geometry), `road-network.js` (`roadGraph`), `buildings.js` (parks), `canopies.js` (open platform, tram-stop and forecourt roofs), `city-map.js` (map layers), `tram-infra.js` (rail and stops) and `trams.js` (track graph and routes).

**Land and water** go through the `city-config.js` land raster (2 m cells over the detail box, 11.6 M of them, built at boot in ~160 ms): `isLand(x, z, margin)` counts bridge decks as land (deck cells are pinned 255 m from water, so a shore margin never narrows a bridge), `isWater` (open water; false on a deck), `distToWater`, `waterAt` (the `WATER` entry under a point; use it to tell a deck over the river from one over land), and `clampToLand(x, z, margin)`, which returns the nearest qualifying point in 2-D as `{x, z}` (always a point; callers needing the margin re-check `isLand`). Outside the detail box is neither land nor water. Also `CITY_BOUNDS` (the detail box, `sidewalkHeight` 0.22, `waterLevel` −3), `RAISED_GROUND` (raised rects the controllers stand on; empty until landmarks push theirs), `districtAt` (osiedle name or null) and `SPAWN` (`{x, z, heading}`: the westbound Św. Marcin carriageway by CK Zamek, facing west).

**Load-time block frontages** are indexed as well ([part 2 record](observations/perf-boot-2-2026-11-11.md)).
`buildings.js` files each block's curb outline edges once in a CSR grid and answers the frontage ray only from the cells
its 18 m segment touches (218 k queries in the 2026-11-11 boot, 6 % crossing within the reach the rule reads), keeps
the frame-transformed landmark and object reserve list per block frame instead of rebuilding it for every lot rect, and
rejects bridge corridors by their world AABB before the separating-axis test. `street-details.js` files each block's
curb edges in a 4 m grid for the lamp clearance and the mapped-tree curb frame; `tram-infra.js` rejects segments by
their bbox in the nearest queries, buckets poles in 4 m cells and tests each viaduct's end box before the ramp distance.
The same arithmetic answers every query; the headless block build fell from 5.1 to 1.9 s and the headless tram/rail
layout from 1.8/2.0 s to 0.66/1.34 s, with every build hash unchanged.

Boot part 4 adds a nearest-distance hierarchy to the same single-block outline cache: median splits and eight-edge
leaves prune by conservative bounds, then run the original squared-distance arithmetic. The cache is replaced on the
next block and cleared after construction. Mapped-tree curb searches now visit only each grid ring's perimeter,
including clipped rings and first-edge distance ties; they no longer walk the square interior just to skip it.
See [the measurements and equivalence checks](observations/perf-boot-4-2026-11-12.md).

Boot part 5 reuses a partial-park block's lot layout across its mapped park rings: `blockLots` is lazy and local to
the current block, rather than repeated for each ring. The signal-placement audit uses numeric cell keys within the
city's coordinate range; its candidate filing and lookup order are unchanged and its grids expire with the audit.
See [the measurements and rejected clearance/culling trials](observations/perf-boot-5-2026-11-12.md).

Street-details clears its private curb-edge, wall, tree-neighbour, tree-lane, mapped-tree and lot-frame placement
indexes after tree construction; its later signal-head audit and light updates do not read them. The railway
collision callback captures only the final raster tiles, outside the layout scope, so it does not retain the lane,
rail and fence construction grids. Together these ownership fixes save about 104 MiB of retained browser heap;
the sites, colliders, network and raster stay intact ([heap investigation](observations/perf-heap-2026-11-11.md)).

Building massing drops its facade staging buckets and roof builder after their instance buffers are filled. Street
furniture drops its temporary tree-site list after `createTreeField` copies it into the LOD arrays, and drops local
references to the tree-pit meshes so culling can replace them without retaining the original instance buffers. The
rendered geometry, tree LOD inputs and streamed `allBlocks` records remain intact. The road network also clears its
edge-deduplication, incoming-edge, paint-node and lane-placement tram indexes after construction. These local
ownership changes and the packed footprint queries save about 43 MiB on both tiers
([second heap investigation](observations/perf-heap-2-2026-11-12.md)).

**Load-time point tests** are indexed ([observation record](observations/perf-boot-predicates-2026-11-11.md)). `blockAt` and buildings.js `insideCurbIsland` share a lazily built 4 m z-slot edge index per block (`createPolygonIndex` / `pointInPolyIndexed` / `pointInBlockPoly`), stored as CSR typed arrays; the ray test visits only edges whose z span can cross the point, with the exact endpoint pairing of `pointInPoly`, so edge and vertex ties stay identical. `pointInPark` buckets the park rings in a 64 m grid and indexes each ring, and the land raster's scanline fill reuses the same edge buckets. `city-grid.js` uses one weak per-polygon index for repeated park/plaza samples, `street-details.js` buckets its plaza rects in 64 m cells before `inPlaza`, and `road-paint.js` buckets crosswalks in 16 m cells before the P-22 4 m keep. On 2026-11-11 this took the boot CPU share of `pointInPoly` from 19-22 % to ~4 %, `pointInPark` from ~5-5.5 % to below 1 %, and boot from 18.4 to 11.9 s at 1× CPU and 109.5 to 70.8 s at 6× CPU (headless Chrome, busy machine; the observation file has the method). The block indexes retain 2.4 MB (CSR, down from 17 MB of small arrays); the park indexes are released after the city-grid build and rebuild lazily if a late caller needs them.

## Build order (main.js `initApp`) and why it is fixed
1. `buildLandmarks(scene)` builds landmark models and gathers their oriented colliders.
2. `buildCityBlocks(scene)` generates building footprints and colliders, keeping buildings off `BRIDGES` decks (+3 m, 4 m past each end). It records `footprintsByBlock` for ground-level footprints.
3. `buildCityGrid(scene, { buildingFootprints, buildingColliders })` receives building footprints (to cut park paving, granite beds, and lawn under buildings without slicing surfaces into fragments) and colliders (to drop plaza planters whose trees would collide with walls before emitting boxes/hedges). It assigns **live `let` exports** that later builders import: `parkTreeSites`, `parkLampSites`, `pavedPlazaRects` and `sidewalkBlocks`.
4. `buildAirport(scene)` draws the Ławica layer (`GEO.aeroway`) after the grid and before the canopies/outskirts. Its terminal walls join `allColliders`; the flat surfaces and lights do not collide.
5. `buildCanopies(scene)` emits the open platform, tram-stop and petrol-station roofs from `GEO.canopies`. Its columns join `allColliders`; the decks are above the ground actors and only block a flying camera. It runs after the grid so the city surfaces exist before the canopies shade them, and before the outskirts/bridges are built.
6. `cityBlocks.populateLeftoverSlabs()` places trees on leftover lot slabs now that `parkTreeSites` is populated.
7. `buildOutskirts(scene)`, then `buildBridges(scene, { sidewalks: cityGrid.sidewalks })`.
8. `buildStreetDetails(scene, { buildingColliders: allColliders })`: it needs the building colliders for the corner name plates. Its own `colliders` join `allColliders` only after step 10, since its footprints already go to the network; so do `cityGrid.propColliders` (square & park furniture and trees).
9. `createTrafficSystem(scene, cityGrid.sidewalks, obstacles, streetDetails.roadNetwork, buildingColliders)` reuses the
   furnished network built during street-tree placement; omitted fourth arguments retain the `buildRoadNetwork` fallback.
   The obstacles are `cityGrid.footprints`, `streetDetails.footprints` and the colliders with no `obb` and `minY < 3.5`.
   The fifth argument is the real building set (`landmarkColliders` + `cityBlocks.colliders`); `pedestrian-routes.js`
   checks it on the sparse fallback connectors. Its 32 m cell index pads OBBs by `0.4 m × √2` (the turned box can reach
   past its world AABB) and ignores colliders whose underside clears the 1.9 m pedestrian. Omitting the argument leaves
   the fallback connectors with the lot-rect check only.
10. `buildRoadPaint(scene, trafficSystem.network, trafficSystem.crosswalks, railClearance)`, then
   `buildTramInfrastructure(scene, trafficSystem.network)`: it needs the lanes to keep clear of, so none of it is a traffic obstacle; its colliders join `allColliders`.
11. `createTramSystem` gets the road network, infrastructure stops and traffic system, then
   `trafficSystem.setTrams(tramSystem)` enables tram interactions. Controllers get `allColliders` (landmarks, blocks,
   canopies, outskirts, bridges, city-grid, street and park furniture, tram infrastructure) plus `tramInfra.railCorridor` as
   their ground-mode keep-out.
12. `setupSceneCulling` runs over the root groups found **by name**: `CityGrid`, `Airport`, `CityBlocks`, `Canopies`, `StreetDetails`, `Landmarks`, `Outskirts`, `Bridges`, `TramInfrastructure`. It chunks cheap props with wider cells, bakes position-only shadow stand-ins for the big static casters, and (high tier only) merges cheap street-prop casters into one stand-in per 500 m cell; low skips that bake. See [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#culling-scene-cullingjs-registering-new-static-geometry).
13. On WebGPU only, instance matrices are swapped for storage attributes; see [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#renderer-and-the-webgl2-fallback).

Per frame: `cityBlocks.update(camX, camZ)` (detail streaming), `cityBlocks.updateLights`, `streetDetails.update(camera)` (pooled lamp lights), `landmarks.update(dt, elapsed, presetId)` (the Ratusz goats, using the shared `city-clock.js` show schedule also read by audio: start on `midday`, repeat every 120 s), and `updateSceneCulling`. Outskirts and bridges have no per-frame update. Trees update themselves from a chained `scene.onBeforeRender` that skips orthographic and array cameras, so shadow passes don't change the levels of detail.

## Road network (shared by traffic, pedestrians and paint)
The [2026-11-12 sweep audit](observations/car-tram-sweep-2026-11-12.md) checks the final lane-end standing positions
against the tram models' articulated section chords. A setback is per edge: clearing one lane is insufficient when
the other lanes still intersect the sweep, and retreat must leave room for a complete car. On the current benchmark
network, Jeżyce 4660/4661 and the three-lane Kaponiera 6522 cannot meet both requirements by retreat alone. The offline
list includes hypothetical retreats beyond an edge; these are diagnostics, not new road or connector geometry.

- The network comes from the OSM centreline graph (`GEO.roadGraph`): junctions (nearby graph nodes merged), straight runs between them and every bend, lanes from each link's real width, OSM lane count and direction. Each edge carries its link's speed limit as `limit` (m/s; from the link's km/h, 50 where it has none), which caps the drivers' desired speed (`docs/actors.md`). Bridge links are ordinary links, because `isLand` counts their decks. It is then **cut by what was built**: lanes must keep clear of the curbs and obstacles city-grid and street-details hand over. If you move curbs or sidewalks in city-grid.js, lanes vanish or reappear.
- **Lane placement** (ports keep lanes on their OSM carriageways;
  `tests/road-network.test.cjs` checks it on the real network with the curbs city-grid hands over):
  - **Ports.** A run into a merged junction (graph nodes under 14 m apart, step 2) ends level with the junction centre
    but on the line of its link's own end: `addEdge` takes the run's `p0`/`p1`, and the edge records how far its line
    passes the nodes (`offFrom`/`offTo`, right of it). An arm's `half` includes that offset, so crossing roads still
    stop clear of both roadways of a divided road. Skewed into the centre, which lies in the median or the tram strip,
    the runs led their lanes across it and queued cars at an angle before the stop line.
  - **Divided roads.** Two one-way roadways between the same two junctions keep their own lines; only roadways drawn
    closer than `PAIR_SEP` (4 m) share the junction centres' line with each way's lanes from 1.8 m right of it. They are
    `divided` and major by both roadways' width either way. One-way roadways the partner pass finds on top of each other
    keep to the right of the line midway between them. Paint pairs two edges into one road only when they run along one
    line.
  - **Tram strips.** A tram link is 6.5 m (the strip) wider than its carriageway. Where the tracks run beside the
    carriageway (link flags bit 3 right / bit 4 left of a→b, `docs/geodata.md`), the roadway is laid out 3.25 m to the
    tracks' side of the link's points, as poz-blocks.mjs stamps it, and the lanes stay on the carriageway (`lanesOf`).
    Centred on every tram link, the strip put 3.25 m of roadway past the real outer curb and the lanes looked shifted
    towards the tracks.
  - **Shared tram lanes.** A single car lane on a flagged shared tram street, without a separate strip,
    may overlap the opposing track because OSM's roadway line is off-centre. At three points along the run, an
    unambiguous pair of street tracks determines the right-hand track; the lane moves there only inside the roadway
    and after `laneOpen` passes. Ambiguous, single-track, segregated and multi-lane cases stay unchanged. This runs
    before stop lines, connectors and paint are built. Rynek Jeżycki's eastbound exit kept less than 1.6 m from the
    opposing track; it now leaves over 2.4 m, with no lost lanes or graph edges. `shared-tram-lanes.test.cjs` checks
    the real differential case, paint/port agreement and the synthetic exclusions.
  - **Opposing strip tracks.** On a two-lane edge (not a shared tram street), where a one-way street track running
    against the edge lies less than 2.5 m to the left of the inner lane at all five sample points along it, the inner
    lane moves to 2.5 m from the nearest such track. It moves only if the two lanes stay at least 2.6 m apart, the lane
    stays `LANE_CLEAR` inside the roadway and `laneOpen` passes (`clearOpposingStripTrack`, road-network.js). Of the 61
    opposing close lanes `tools/audit-tram-opposing-lanes.mjs` listed at `1eff3b4`, this moves only Półwiejska's
    southbound inner lane (edge 7500, offset 1.45 → 2.34 m: it ran within 1.62 m of track 275 over 85 m and now keeps
    over the audit's 2.3 m). `shared-tram-lanes.test.cjs` checks its clearance, lane spacing and paint.

    placed and resolves lanes against the mapped street tracks instead of the nominal `TRAM_W` strip. On right-hand

    oncoming, and a two-way piece with no partner can be. Where a lane stays under 2.3 m of such a track for 10 m or
    more, all of the edge's lanes move together (their spacing stays, and a two-way link's directed edge keeps its lanes
    on its own side of the centreline) by the least amount that clears it, on the car part of the carriageway and with
    `laneOpen` passing. A strip road must not leave a lane on a same-direction track; a track no `route=tram` relation

The 4.4 m-grown rect cover (`sidewalkBlocks`, for rect consumers) reaches into the roadway on turned blocks; passed as curbs, it cut the largest strongly connected set from ~3150 to ~1660 edges.
- `buildRoadNetwork` returns `{ nodes, edges, paintEdges, connector, curvePoint, walkable, isRoadClear, configureCrosswalks }`.
  `configureCrosswalks` precomputes compound-crossing connector and rear-clearance reservations after pedestrian
  layout; see actors.md. It does not alter lane geometry or stop lines.
  - Edges are straight and directed. Each has a unit direction `(dx, dz)`, a right normal `(rx, rz) = (−dz, dx)`, and `lanes` as lateral offsets right of the centreline, sorted left to right. `paintEdges` carry `limit`, and their nodes `signal` (set at step 6).
  - Nodes have `in`/`out` edges, `arms`, `signal`, and `shape` for bend points (one road going on, < 35°: no corner, no crosswalk).
  - Step 7 assigns sides and right-of-way groups: a node's sides are its arms, numbered from 0; the arms of one road form a group, and at unsignalised junctions group 0 is the widest road (`axis` `'ave'` marks major roads). At signals, arm groups instead match the original displayed arm in the shared local plan, even when lane centring rotates the runtime arm. An edge's `axisIndex` is its arm's group, except into an unsignalised node with two arms (a bend, 387 of them bent more than 35°, so two "roads"): there it is 0 both ways, since nothing crosses there (one way round used to give way to the other).
  - Lane-to-lane curves are built lazily by `connector(e, i, f, j)` and cached.
  - Only the largest strongly connected component is kept, so no vehicle can reach a dead end. A blocked lane can make whole streets vanish from traffic. The build logs `[road-network] edges: N with clear lanes, M long enough, K in the largest strongly connected set`; watch K after touching curbs or obstacles. `tests/road-network.test.cjs` pins the bare graph (no curbs or furniture) at > 12,500 long-enough edges and > 8,200 edges / 410 km in the set, and requires street furniture to preserve ≥ 99% of its span. Current bare measurement: 13,103 long enough, 8,756 edges / 435.5 km in the set. The production-aligned grid-and-furniture variant is measured separately below. The historical smaller-box baseline was 4,235 edges / 199.3 km. Runs get a shape node wherever the real centreline strays > 0.5 m from the chord (not within 25 m of a junction): with bends-only shape nodes, gentle arcs strayed ~3 m and the outer lanes hit the curbside lamps, which cut the set to 127 km.
- **Obstacle rule:** an obstacle footprint anywhere within `LANE_CLEAR` (1.2 m) of a lane, between two nodes, removes that lane from the whole edge. Keep obstacle footprints on curb islands, or deliberately in medians.
- For pedestrians, `walkable(minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ)` rejects non-curb footprints unless they are explicitly marked `walkable` (the grid's median surfaces). Those surfaces still block cars; the median outline checks and all other obstacles are unchanged. `pedestrian-routes.js` uses `walkable` and `blockAt`.
- **Signals:** `SIGNAL_SITES` (city-config) is the one list of signalised junctions, built from `GEO.roadGraph.signals` (490 OSM signal nodes in the 8.2 × 5.7 km box), `{x, z, nsWidth, ewWidth, junction, arms}` each (`nsWidth`/`ewWidth`: the widest arm running mostly north–south and east–west; `junction`/`arms`: the graph nodes merged into one junction as in step 2, and the links leaving it, for the signal heads). Step 6 marks the node within `SIGNAL_REACH` 12 m of each site, and `street-details.js` section 1 mounts signal heads at each junction (it runs before the network exists, so it can't read `node.signal`). Lenses, traffic and audio share per-junction `SIGNAL_PLANS` via `site.timing` / `node.signalTiming`; see actors.md for timing, demand crossings and offsets, and rendering.md for the shared display texture. The `signalClock` uniform is set every frame by main.js. Signals run two phases: group 0 (the plan's main axis; `isAvenue` in `signalPhase`/`walkSignal`) and group 1. Each runs green, amber, red and then, as in Poland, red and amber together (`'redAmber'`, the last 2 s of its red, after the other group's 3 s amber) before green; the lens material lights both lamps then, and the pedestrian heads stay red.
- `road-paint.js` draws on `network.paintEdges` and uses `isRoadClear` to drop centre lines that would cross obstacles. ## Street furniture (`street-details.js`)
Built before the road network, so everything that stands near a lane is checked against `roadClearance(x, z)` (city-config): the distance outside the nearest roadway as road-network.js lays its runs out (`LANE_GUIDE`). Near merged junctions those runs stray up to ~2 m from the real centrelines `nearestRoads` measures, so a curb-outline test alone left poles in lanes. `clearOfRoads(x, z, r)` asks for `r + 0.3` m. `LANE_GUIDE` holds, per link:
  - the link from merged junction centre to junction centre through its real bends, at its own half width, or wider where the lanes' `LANE_CLEAR` reaches past the curbs (at least 1.2 m on a one-way, a quarter of the width + 1.2 m on a two-way: the 3.3 m streets);
  - the chords road-network.js cuts near junctions, where it places no shape node (`JUNCTION_END` 25 m): from each junction centre to the points 15 and 25 m along, and the whole link under 50 m;
  - for a one-way link with an opposite one-way link of the same name nearby, a 1.2 m lane strip 1.8 m right of it: step 3 used to put every such pair onto one centreline with each way's lanes from 1.8 m right (now only pairs drawn under 4 m apart; the strip stays as a superset).
  Links with a tram strip beside them (flags bits 3/4) are moved 3.25 m to the tracks' side, as road-network.js lays their roadway out.
  Without the last three, 18 LED poles, 6 bollards and 2 parking machines cost 2.3, 1.1 and 0.9 km: the lanes they stood in lay up to 2 m outside the old guide.
- **Crosswalk landings.** `JUNCTIONS` (city-config: every merged junction with its centre and arms) gives each arm's crosswalk: the `CROSSWALK` band `STOP_SETBACK` inside a stop line estimated as in `stopBack` from the arms crossing it (both imported from road-network.js; the estimate does not squeeze short links), from curb island to curb island: like pedestrian-routes.js, each side runs on past the arm's curb line to the first `blockAt` within `CROSSWALK_REACH`. ## Tram infrastructure
`tram-infra.js` builds everything static about the trams from `GEO.rail` (`kind: 'tram'`) and `GEO.tramStops`, and the mainline railway from the `kind: 'rail'` tracks (see **Mainline railway** below), after the road network. The placements are the pure `layoutTramInfrastructure(network)` and `layoutRailInfrastructure(network)` (no three.js), which `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` runs on the real network.
- **Rails:** each track is cut into straight pieces of up to 4 m (`PIECE`), so it follows bends and curbs. Each piece gets two instanced rail heads, 70 mm wide at 1435 mm gauge, and a 42 mm dark groove inside each head. There is no tram track bed: every piece lies on the asphalt at ground level (the y 0 land deck), or on a curb island's 0.22 m paving, with the heads flush with that surface. The head top is y 0.016 and the grooves 0.014, both above the road paint (0.012), which keeps them from z-fighting and puts them over the markings. The rail is 0.05 m tall about that top, so its foot stays buried in the ground deck off the street instead of floating. `layoutTrackRails` returns the pieces (`pieces` in the layout); pieces over water with no deck are dropped. `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` checks that no `TramBallast` mesh is built and that every piece sits on the ground or a curb island. The mainline railway keeps its own granite beds and sleepers (see **Mainline railway** below).
- **Overhead line:** a contact wire at `WIRE_Y` 5.8 m runs along every track polyline. Supports stand every ~30 m (`STATION`) along each track, unless one already carries that track within 12 m. From the track, `march` looks out on both sides for the first clear spot: on land, ≥ 2 m from every lane centreline and turning path (`laneDistance` also files the network's connector curves: on lanes alone, poles stood on 15 turns and cost 27 edges) and ≥ 1.75 m from every track. It prefers a curb, with the pole 0.6 m behind the curb edge. If no curb is found, it takes the first clear spot, which can be a centre pole between two tracks or a pole on a reservation. Where both sides are found, a street track gets two 7.6 m poles with a span wire at 6.75 m. Within 900 m of the Ratusz, streets with under 15 m between the two spots get wall rosettes 3.8 m behind the curbs instead (an approximation of the house fronts). Otherwise the nearest side within 9 m gets a pole with a cantilever arm and a stay. Off-street track only looks 5 m out. Droppers hang wherever a span or arm crosses a track. Poles join the player colliders.
- **Stops:** each stop node snaps to the nearest tram track within 25 m. Nodes within 20 m on one track merge. The platform goes on the right of the travel direction: on one-way track, the right of its points; on two-way track, the side away from a parallel track 2–8 m off (both sides are tried if there is none).
  - **Island:** a platform 1.35 m from the track centreline, 33 m long (shorter on short chains, ≥ 18 m), 2.6, 2.0 or 1.5 m wide, 0.25 m high, with a yellow tactile strip. It is curved along the track as 3 m pieces on the curb material. Every sample must be on land, ≥ 1.75 m from every lane centreline (`LANE_KEEP`; road-network's box test reaches that far on diagonal streets) and clear of other tracks.
  - **Curb stop:** if no island fits, the stop stands on a sidewalk within 12 m of the track. Passengers cross the lane to board, as at the real Poznań stops that have no island.
  - **Furniture:** a glass shelter (with a back-lit advert panel that lights by `uInteriorGlow`) when 2 m or more of depth is free, a stop sign with a name plate, and a ticket machine. The name plates come from one canvas atlas (a yellow "TRAMWAJ" band over the Polish name on ZTM green; no logos), indexed by a per-instance `plateCell`; each cell is 128 × 64 px on the 0.8 × 0.4 m plate (6.25 mm per texel, the old 256 × 128 cell cost 17.3 MB). Shelters, signs and machines join the player colliders as oriented boxes (`obb`), since they stand turned with the track: their old world AABBs made a diagonal shelter's local depth `hx + hz`, up to 2.28 m past the glass (fixed 2026-11-04). The shelter's back face keeps 0.08 m behind the glass at local z = −0.62, its front stops at +0.7 m just past the front posts and inside the 0.8 m roof edge (the bench fills the back half, so the open side stays blocked under the roof), and its ends match the end panel / advert at len/2 (the longer roof is above player height). The ticket machine uses its 0.62 × 0.48 m top cap (0.31 × 0.24 m half extents); the sign keeps its 0.08 m pole box because the 0.82 m plate is above the player. `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` checks every collider against the rendered dimensions for both shelter lengths and both stop kinds. Platforms do not collide, so the player can drive and walk across them.

- **Mainline railway:** the 526 `rail` tracks (127.2 km) are cut into straight pieces of up to 8 m (`RAIL_PIECE`; a 300 m curve is 27 mm off the chord). The world is flat and Poznań's railway crosses most streets and every tram line on bridges and in underpasses: a piece is dropped when its centreline (sampled at its ends, quarters and middle) comes within 3.5 m of a roadway (`RAIL_ROAD_KEEP`, the 2.5 m half bed + 1 m; `roadClearance` over the `LANE_GUIDE` carriageways, junction boxes included, and the network's lane centrelines less 1.75 m; under a `BRIDGES` deck counts as on the road) or within 5 m of a tram track centreline (`RAIL_TRAM_KEEP`: half bed + 1 m + the tram's 1.5 m swept body).   - **Grade-separated railway crossings and viaducts:** Poznań's mainline railway crosses streets on bridges and in underpasses.   - **Corridor boundary:** along the outer edge of each track bundle, 3.6 m from the outer track's centreline (`RAIL_FENCE_O`, 1.1 m past the bed's foot), a concrete plinth carries a 2 m welded-mesh fence (`RailFence`: a plane per segment, PKP grey-green, its `maskNode` drawing posts every 2.5 m, top and bottom rails and a 5 × 20 cm mesh that turns into a 25% 2×2 screen-door once a wire is under a pixel; per-instance `fence` = segment length and arc length along the run, so the posts keep their rhythm). A piece's side is an outer edge when no track centre lies within 5.5 m of a point 3 m beyond the fence (at its middle and ends); each of its five samples must be on land, ≥ 1 m outside every roadway and off road decks (`RAIL_FENCE_ROAD`), ≥ 3 m from every tram track and ≥ 3 m from every rail track centreline (off every bed). Vertices sit at the piece joints on the mitred normal, so consecutive pieces form one run with no gaps or overlaps at bends; run ends within 2 m of each other (across a switch, into another track chain) are joined at their midpoint. Runs stop at cuts against the end walls. A run end within 8 m (`RAIL_STUB`) of another run's end or an end wall's corner gets a straight stub to it when the stub keeps the same clearances (historically 46 stubs in the smaller box). Where a road, a block, a plaza or open land with no track within 6 m lies 3–12 m beyond (the street alongside, the station car parks, which are unmapped ground), the plinth is a 1.2 m wall (`RAIL_WALL_H`) under the fence; between parallel bundles and along the water it is 0.5 m (`RAIL_PLINTH_H`). Plinths and walls go in `RailEndWalls`. Each segment is an oriented player collider up to the fence top. Masts keep 0.4 m off the boundary. Current layout: 456 boundary runs. Historical smaller-box measurement: 266 runs, 3,762 segments, 29.6 km (25.9 km over a wall; 5.6 km in the Poznań Główny box, 2.7 km of it walled).
  - **Player keep-out:** the ground modes may not enter the corridor at all, fence or no fence. `layoutRailInfrastructure` rasterises it at 1 m in 64 m tiles kept only where there is corridor (`corridor.has(x, z)`, allocation-free; `buildTramInfrastructure` returns it as `railCorridor`, which main.js hands to `ThirdPersonControllerManager` as `keepOut`). Each rectangle is filled row by row from the analytic column bounds (the exact per-cell predicate is checked only at the two ends of the run), so the 1 m cells are written in spans instead of testing every cell centre. It is each kept piece's rectangle 3.4 m either side (`RAIL_KEEPOUT`, just inside the 3.6 m boundary, so where there is a fence its collider stops a body first), 0.25 m past both ends (under the end walls), plus, in 2 m steps, the strip out to a neighbouring track on any side that fails the boundary's outer-edge test (so the strips between bundles too close to fence are closed and nothing reaches past a fence), unless the way across comes within 3 m of a roadway or 2.5 m of a tram track. Since kept pieces stay 3.5 m from every roadway, the roads crossing the cuts stay open. Current keep-out: 531,858 raster cells. Historical smaller-box clearance check: no roadway point beyond the 143 cut ends was inside; 2 of 3,762 points 0.8 m outside the fence were (a fence 3.1 m from a neighbouring track, a stub). See [architecture.md](architecture.md) for the controllers' side. Each piece lies on a trapezoid granite bed (`RailBallast`: 5 m at the foot, 4 m on top at 0.30 m, plus a few mm per track so overlapping beds at switches don't z-fight) whose shader draws 2.6 m concrete sleepers every 0.6 m from a per-instance `tie` attribute; at 4.5 m track centres the beds close up into one yard surface. Two 60E1 heads (0.16 m) stand on top. Every track is taken as electrified (PKP 3 kV DC; no `electrified` tag in the geodata): a contact wire at 5.6 m and a messenger at 7.0 m run as straight chords over up to three consecutive kept pieces, never across a cut. Each cut end gets a support 1.5 m back (else 4 or 8 m); then supports every ~55 m (`RAIL_STATION`) along each track unless one already carries it within 22 m: a mast (0.3 m box section, 8.2 m) on the first clear spot within 7 m of the track with a sloping cantilever tube and a registration arm; failing that (inside a yard), a portal: two 9.6 m masts on the first clear spots up to 40 m out on both sides and a 0.5 m beam at 8.9 m with droppers to every track it crosses. A clear spot is on land, ≥ 2 m outside every roadway (`RAIL_MAST_ROAD`), ≥ 3 m from every tram track (`RAIL_MAST_TRAM`), ≥ 2.6 m from every rail track, and on a block only within 3.5 m of its curb; neither an arm nor a portal beam may reach over a roadway or a tram track. Current layout: 1,558 masts, 1,322 cantilevers and 146 portals. Historical smaller-box check: every piece had a support within 45 m, and 1 of the 143 cut ends none within 10 m. Masts join the player colliders; their footprints and the boundary's (sampled every 2 m) were checked like the tram ones in the historical smaller-box obstacle experiment (4,282 edges / 193.1 km). Rail heads, wires, masts and arms go into the tram meshes (`TramRails`, `CatenaryWires`, `CatenaryArms`), so the railway adds three instanced meshes (`RailBallast`, `RailEndWalls`, `RailFence`). The layout takes ~1.1 s headless on the 13.6 × 5.7 km box (2026-11-12; lane, road and track lookups dominate). `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` checks the clearances, the end walls at every cut, the masts, the boundary (off roads, tram tracks and beds; fenced along the yard) and the keep-out (the beds in, the roads at the cuts and the ground outside the fence out); `tests/controllers.test.cjs` the controllers' keep-out. Not drawn by `tram-infra.js`: platforms, station roofs, signals, points (switch blades), the railway land itself (the
`landuse=railway` rings are not exported; beds only cover the tracks), embankments. The open station and platform
roofs are drawn separately from `GEO.canopies` by `canopies.js`. Level crossing barriers, underpass sidewalks and railway viaduct decks over roadways are drawn and collide.
- **Public surface:** `stops` (`{ name, x, z, heading, railIndex, s, dir, side, kind }`). `x, z` is the stop point on the track. `s` is the arc length along `GEO.rail[railIndex].pts`. `dir` is +1 when trams serving it run along the points. `heading` is their travel heading (0 = +z). `kind` is `'island'` or `'curb'`. The tram actors can dwell there.

## Batching
- `batchStaticGroupMeshes(root, excludeMaterials)` merges all non-instanced meshes that share a **material object** into one mesh. It keeps only `position`, `normal` and `uv`, and meshes missing any of those are skipped. The originals are removed, so their names, userData and custom attributes are lost.
  - Pass a material in `excludeMaterials` to keep its meshes separate, for example a material with custom attributes, or one whose mesh another module looks up.
  - city-grid.js batches its group at the end of `buildCityGrid`, and landmarks.js at the end of `buildLandmarks` (the `LandmarksDynamic` root is left out).
- Share material instances wherever possible. Every distinct node material is another WGSL pipeline to precompile behind the loading screen, which is why landmarks share "canonical" facade materials.
- landmarks.js re-emits unrotated `BoxGeometry` parts that use a material from `boxLocalTwins` as instanced facade boxes (`createFacadeBoxes`, tsl-materials.js).

2. Call `addFootprint(x, z, w, d, kind)` for each prop that should block pedestrians and lanes, and mind the obstacle rule above: check `clearOfRoads`, and keep it within ~1 m of the curb or beyond 3.8 m (the walking lines run 1.7 and 2.8 m in, ±0.4 m, `pedestrian-routes.js`).
3. Build one `InstancedMesh` per geometry and material, and `group.add` it. It is chunk-culled automatically if it passes the thresholds above. Do not set `frustumCulled = false`.
4. For a night light, push a site onto `streetPointSites` (pooled point lights) or `ledLampSites` (pooled spots).
5. For player collision, push a box onto `colliders` (`addCollider(x, z, hx, hz, maxY)`, or an `obb` collider for a turned prop); main.js adds them to `allColliders`. Also keep it off the crosswalks (`clearOfCrosswalks`).
6. For a sound source, follow how `audioSignals` is consumed by `CityAudioSystem`.
7. A new module needs the registration steps in `AGENTS.md`.

**New landmark:**
1. Footprint: add its OSM name to `tools/data/q_poz_landmarks.overpassql` and its curated entry to `tools/poz-extra.mjs`, refetch and rebuild ([`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). It then appears in `LANDMARK_SITES[name]`.
2. The area module (`landmarks-<area>.js`, conventions in the `landmark-kit.js` header): add a block `{ const c0 = colliders.length; …model around a local origin…; group.add(obj); collider(colliders, …); fitToSite([obj], site('Name'), colliders, c0, { height }); }`. `fitToSite` scales the model to the real height, capped by the outline width (+6%), and moves it onto the site centre. It applies the same transform to every collider pushed since `c0`, so author colliders in the model's own coordinates, as axis-aligned boxes. When the site has an `ang`, the model is scaled in the site's own frame and then turned by `ang` about the site centre, `offset` turns with it, and each collider becomes an `obb` collider (the shape buildings.js `pushCollider` emits), which keeps it out of the traffic obstacles. Turned box parts keep the world-space window grid, because the `boxLocalTwins` re-emit skips rotated parts. Reuse the shared facade materials, and use the `boxLocalTwins` keys for box parts.
3. Add the name to `LANDMARK_RESERVE` in buildings.js, or generic buildings will grow through it.
4. Add it to `CITY_LANDMARKS` (city-config.js) for its map pin and the location label; `tests/city-text.test.cjs` fails while a `LANDMARK_SITES` entry has no pin. Optionally add a `TELEPORT_LOCATIONS` arrival.
5. Towers outside the detail box belong in outskirts.js instead (coordinates via `LL()`).

**New streamed building detail:** add a list to `blockDetail` in `populateBlock`, fill it in `commit()`, add it to `placeInFrame`, create a capped pool next to the `MAX_*` constants, and fill it and set its count and `flagRange` in `update()`.

## Determinism
- World building uses no `Math.random`. Only traffic.js and pedestrian-routes.js do.
  - buildings.js reseeds `mulberry32` per block from `(minX, minZ)` at the start of `populateBlock`. A block's buildings therefore depend only on its own bounds and profile: editing one block leaves the others unchanged, but any geodata change to a block's rect reshuffles that block.
  - outskirts.js and trees.js use `mulberry32` with fixed seeds per generator; city-grid.js and street-details.js hash positions (`prand`, `hashPos`); bridges.js uses no randomness.
- Keep new procedural placement on a seeded generator, so screenshots and tests stay reproducible.
- Draw order matters in the shared generators. Inserting `rand()` calls into an existing loop changes everything generated after it in that seed's scope.

## Road crossings on paved plazas

Paved plazas are cut around crossing drivable `GEO.roadGraph` segments using their recorded half-widths; the
0.3 m paving inset also follows the cut edges. Building holes are retained while the slab is split into convex
pieces. Plaza lamps, benches and planters keep a 0.1 m margin beyond the corridor, accounting for their footprints.
The current graph cuts five segments across three plazas, including Półwiejska / Ogrodowa, the south end of
Półwiejska and Ślusarska / Plac Kolegiacki. Skwer Kaspra Goskiego and the plaza near (4075, −1807) have no
drivable graph road at those locations; another segment crosses the latter plaza near (4408, −955). The regression
checks slab pieces and plaza furniture against crossing graph segments. Browser appearance remains unverified.
