# World building at runtime

Frame: x = east, z = south (north = −z), y up, 1 unit = 1 m, origin at the Ratusz; the axes are not turned (`GEO.frame.bearingDeg` 0, true north up; see [`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). Geodata reaches the world builders mostly through `js/city-config.js` exports (`BLOCKS`, `ROADS`, `PLAZAS`, `LAND_RINGS`/`LAND_HOLES`, `WATER`, `OUTSKIRTS`, `BRIDGES`, `LANDMARK_SITES`, `SIGNAL_SITES`). Only `road-network.js` (`GEO.roadGraph`), `buildings.js` (`GEO.parks`), `city-map.js` (`frame`, `parks`) and `tram-infra.js` (`GEO.rail`, `GEO.tramStops`) import `GEO` directly.

**Land and water** go through the `city-config.js` land raster (2 m cells over the detail box, built at boot in ~40 ms): `isLand(x, z, margin)` counts bridge decks as land (deck cells are pinned 255 m from water, so a shore margin never narrows a bridge), `isWater` (open water; false on a deck), `distToWater`, `waterAt` (the `WATER` entry under a point; use it to tell a deck over the river from one over land), and `clampToLand(x, z, margin)`, which returns the nearest qualifying point in 2-D as `{x, z}` (always a point; callers needing the margin re-check `isLand`). Outside the detail box is neither land nor water. Also `CITY_BOUNDS` (the detail box, `sidewalkHeight` 0.22, `waterLevel` −3), `RAISED_GROUND` (raised rects the controllers stand on; empty until landmarks push theirs), `districtAt` (osiedle name or null) and `SPAWN` (`{x, z, heading}`: the westbound Św. Marcin carriageway by CK Zamek, facing west).

## Map (non-obvious ownership)
- `city-grid.js` `buildCityGrid`: the water plane at `waterLevel`, the street-level land deck (`LAND_RINGS` minus the ponds in `LAND_HOLES`), battered stone embankments from the deck edge down below the water, raised sidewalks and curbs of every `BLOCKS` curb island (instanced slabs for rect islands, extruded outlines for the rest), hedged medians, the paved squares (`PLAZAS`, deduplicated, minus outlines carrying > 30 m of road centreline) and the park blocks (lawn, trees, lamps, ponds cut out). It also exports `batchStaticGroupMeshes`, which landmarks.js reuses.
- `outskirts.js` `buildOutskirts(scene)` → `{ group, colliders: [] }`: everything between the detail box and the city boundary (`OUTSKIRTS`, lakes as holes), a horizon filler beyond the city line, the land-use "city carpet" shader with per-zone street grids, and instanced buildings on the zone grids. The osiedle zones (Winogrady, Piątkowo, Rataje, Chartowo, Żegrze, plus a share of Grunwald, Ogrody and Naramowice blocks) get wielka płyta superblocks: 5-storey slabs 60–120 m long, 11-storey slabs and 16-storey point blocks (2.8 m storeys + a 1.2 m attic), at least `panelGap` = max(18 m, 0.9 × height) apart and clear of zone borders, on lawns with tree clumps and footpaths that the ground shader paints for osiedle zones. They are `createFacadeBoxes` boxes on `createPanelBlockMaterial` (`boxLocal`), with windowless gable ends and stairwell doors on one long side; five finishes (`PANEL_FINISHES`: grey, cream and three retrofit accents) share one pipeline, chunked at 3.6 km, so each chunk costs one draw per finish (about 60 in all). Villas, plastered blocks and sheds stay unit boxes on the cheap `createBoroughBuildingMaterial`, chunked at 1.2 km. Hand-placed land use is written in lon/lat through `LL()`. Nothing collides: ground modes never leave the detail box.
- `bridges.js` `buildBridges(scene, { sidewalks })` → `{ group, colliders }`: one structure per `BRIDGES` name (links merged across carriageways and junction pieces), as a tied arch, a girder/box beam or an overpass (`KIND_BY_NAME`, else by whether `waterAt` finds water under it). The roadway deck sits at y ≈ −0.01 on the flat road graph and runs `DECK_REACH` 15 m onto each bank; the player collides only with the parapets (oriented boxes). `sidewalks` is accepted but unused so far.
- `buildings.js` `buildCityBlocks`: generic buildings on every block's lot rects as Poznań perimeter blocks (corner houses, rows of 4–5-storey kamienice with oficyny round the courtyards; the Rynek's pastel houses, the Dzielnica Cesarska's sandstone public buildings, offices, villas, works and osiedle panel slabs by district), plus the streamed roof and street-level detail. `balconies.js` is that detail's near/far wrought-iron balcony renderer.
- `landmarks.js` `buildLandmarks(scene)` → `{ group, colliders, update }`: the hand-modelled Poznań landmarks, one area module each (`landmarks-rynek.js`: Ratusz with the noon koziołki, Domki Budnicze, Wieża Wójtowska; `landmarks-heritage.js`: Katedra, Fara, Zamek Królewski, Brama Poznania; `landmarks-centrum.js`: Zamek Cesarski, Teatr Wielki, Collegium Minus, Okrąglak, the 1956 crosses; `landmarks-dworzec.js`: Poznań Główny, Stary Dworzec, Avenida, Stary Browar, Iglica; `landmarks-towers.js`: Bałtyk and the skyline towers). They share `landmark-kit.js`: `fitToSite`, the canonical materials (`createLandmarkMaterials`), the facade-box pass and roof/lathe/prism helpers, with the modelling conventions in its header. Static parts go into the `Landmarks` root; moving parts (the goats) into a separate `LandmarksDynamic` root that is neither batched nor culled. `street-details.js`: signal heads, street name plates, streetlights, curbside furniture, kiosks and advertising columns, and all trees (it plants them through `trees.js` `createTreeField`); see [Street furniture](#street-furniture-street-detailsjs).
- `road-network.js`: the drivable and walkable graph. `traffic.js` builds it; it is not built in main.js. `road-paint.js`: road markings generated from that graph.
- `tram-infra.js` `buildTramInfrastructure(scene, network)` → `{ group, colliders, stops }`: the static tram infrastructure (Phase 9 step 1), see [Tram infrastructure](#tram-infrastructure).

## Build order (main.js `initApp`) and why it is fixed
1. `buildCityGrid(scene)` comes first. It assigns **live `let` exports** that later builders import: `parkTreeSites`, `parkLampSites`, `pavedPlazaRects`, `sidewalkBlocks`, `parkClearings` (the ponds; nothing reads it yet) and `keepClearBoxes` (`[{minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ}]` no building may stand in; empty for now). They are `undefined` until it has run.
2. `buildOutskirts(scene)`, then `buildBridges(scene, { sidewalks: cityGrid.sidewalks })`.
3. `buildLandmarks(scene)`.
4. `buildCityBlocks` keeps buildings off the `BRIDGES` decks (+3 m, 4 m past each end) and `keepClearBoxes`, and reads the plaza, tree and sidewalk exports.
5. `buildStreetDetails(scene, { buildingColliders: allColliders })`: it needs the building colliders for the corner name plates.
6. `createTrafficSystem(scene, cityGrid.sidewalks, obstacles)` → `buildRoadNetwork`. The obstacles are `cityGrid.footprints`, `streetDetails.footprints` and the colliders with no `obb` and `minY < 3.5`.
7. `buildRoadPaint(scene, trafficSystem.network)`, then `buildTramInfrastructure(scene, trafficSystem.network)`: it needs the lanes to keep clear of, so none of it is a traffic obstacle; its colliders join `allColliders`.
8. Controllers get `allColliders`: landmarks + blocks + outskirts + bridges + city-grid + tram infrastructure.
9. `setupSceneCulling` runs over the root groups found **by name**: `CityGrid`, `CityBlocks`, `StreetDetails`, `Landmarks`, `Outskirts`, `Bridges`, `TramInfrastructure`.
10. On WebGPU only, instance matrices are swapped for storage attributes; see [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#renderer-and-the-webgl2-fallback).

Per frame: `cityBlocks.update(camX, camZ)` (detail streaming), `cityBlocks.updateLights`, `streetDetails.update(camera)` (pooled lamp lights), `landmarks.update(dt, elapsed, presetId)` (the Ratusz goats, keyed to the `midday` lighting preset), and `updateSceneCulling`. Outskirts and bridges have no per-frame update. Trees update themselves from a chained `scene.onBeforeRender` that skips orthographic and array cameras, so shadow passes don't change the levels of detail.

## Road network (shared by traffic, pedestrians and paint)
- The network comes from the OSM centreline graph (`GEO.roadGraph`): junctions (nearby graph nodes merged), straight runs between them and every bend, lanes from each link's real width, OSM lane count and direction. Each edge carries its link's speed limit as `limit` (m/s; from the link's km/h, 50 where it has none), which caps the drivers' desired speed (`docs/actors.md`). Bridge links are ordinary links, because `isLand` counts their decks. It is then **cut by what was built**: lanes must keep clear of the curbs and obstacles city-grid and street-details hand over. If you move curbs or sidewalks in city-grid.js, lanes vanish or reappear.
- **Curbs:** city-grid passes rect islands as they are and irregular islands as their **ungrown** lot rects. The 4.4 m-grown rect cover (`sidewalkBlocks`, for rect consumers) reaches into the roadway on turned blocks; passed as curbs, it cut the largest strongly connected set from ~3150 to ~1660 edges.
- `buildRoadNetwork` returns `{ nodes, edges, paintEdges, connector, curvePoint, walkable, isRoadClear }`.
  - Edges are straight and directed. Each has a unit direction `(dx, dz)`, a right normal `(rx, rz) = (−dz, dx)`, and `lanes` as lateral offsets right of the centreline, sorted left to right. `bike` is `[left, right]`: a painted bike lane along that curb in its direction of travel (link flags bits 3/4, OSM `cycleway=lane`); the left one is dropped where the left curb became a median (divided roads). `paintEdges` carry `bike` and `limit`, and their nodes `signal` (set at step 6).
  - Nodes have `in`/`out` edges, `arms`, `signal`, and `shape` for bend points (one road going on, < 35°: no corner, no crosswalk).
  - Step 7 assigns sides and right-of-way groups: a node's sides are its arms, numbered from 0; the arms of one road form a group, and group 0 is the widest road (`axis` `'ave'` marks major roads).
  - Lane-to-lane curves are built lazily by `connector(e, i, f, j)` and cached.
  - Only the largest strongly connected component is kept, so no vehicle can reach a dead end. A blocked lane can make whole streets vanish from traffic. The build logs `[road-network] edges: N with clear lanes, M long enough, K in the largest strongly connected set`; watch K after touching curbs or obstacles. `tests/road-network.test.cjs` pins it on the real land: the bare graph (no curbs or furniture) at > 6200 long enough and > 3850 edges / 175 km in the set (measured 6925, 4300 / 193.8 km), and street-details' furniture must leave ≥ 90% of those km (measured 184.6 km with 15,284 footprints; furniture keeps outside `roadClearance`, see Street furniture below). Runs get a shape node wherever the real centreline strays > 0.5 m from the chord (not within 25 m of a junction): with bends-only shape nodes, gentle arcs strayed ~3 m and the outer lanes hit the curbside lamps, which cut the set to 127 km.
- **Obstacle rule:** an obstacle footprint anywhere within `LANE_CLEAR` (1.2 m) of a lane, between two nodes, removes that lane from the whole edge. Keep obstacle footprints on curb islands, or deliberately in medians.
- For pedestrians, `walkable(minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ)` rejects any non-curb footprint. `pedestrian-routes.js` uses `walkable` and `blockAt`.
- **Signals:** `SIGNAL_SITES` (city-config) is the one list of signalised junctions, built from `GEO.roadGraph.signals` (235 OSM signal nodes), `{x, z, nsWidth, ewWidth, junction, arms}` each (`nsWidth`/`ewWidth`: the widest arm running mostly north–south and east–west; `junction`/`arms`: the graph nodes merged into one junction as in step 2, and the links leaving it, for the signal heads). Step 6 marks the node within `SIGNAL_REACH` 12 m of each site, and `street-details.js` section 1 mounts signal heads at each junction (it runs before the network exists, so it can't read `node.signal`). Lenses and traffic both run on `SIGNAL_TIMING` (city-config) and the `signalClock` uniform, which main.js sets every frame. Signals run two phases: group 0 (the widest road; `isAvenue` in `signalPhase`/`walkSignal`) and every other group. Each runs green, amber, red and then, as in Poland, red and amber together (`'redAmber'`, the last `redAmber` 2 s of its red, while the other group is on amber) before green; the lens material lights both lamps then, and the pedestrian heads stay red.
- `road-paint.js` draws on `network.paintEdges` and uses `isRoadClear` to drop centre lines that would cross obstacles. It imports `CROSSWALK` (4.3 m between the intersection box and the stop lines: a 4.0 m P-10 zebra plus 0.15 m each side) from road-network.js, which uses the same constant for the stop-line setback. Change it in that one place. Raising it from 2.5 m left the strongly connected set unchanged (4300 edges, 193.8 km of span) and shortened the stop-line-to-stop-line lane length from 160.6 to 156.7 km.
- **Markings** (Polish, all white): P-1 dashed lines (0.12 m, 4 m dashes, 8 m gaps) between lanes going the same way; between opposing lanes P-4 double solid where either way has ≥ 2 lanes, P-1 dashed on a one-lane-each-way road above 30 km/h and nothing at 30 km/h or less (residential streets); P-14 stop lines (0.5 m deep, 1 m rectangles 0.5 m apart) only on approaches to signalised junctions, since the geodata has no STOP or give-way signs for P-12/P-13; P-10 zebras 4.0 m deep in 0.5 m stripes across every junction approach; red bike lanes (up to 1.5 m, 0.2 m off the curb, edged by a 0.24 m solid line) along the curbs `edge.bike` marks, only where a car in the outer lane would keep off the line (≥ 1 m of bike lane). Measured on the furnished network: 17.6 km of edge span carries a bike-lane side (15.2 km of tagged curb in the graph), of which 4.1 km get paint (most tagged streets have their lanes spread to the curbs); 368 stop-line pieces; about 1.8 km of P-4 line (most multi-lane roads are divided, so their centre is a median). No "BUS" lane text (no bus-lane data).

## Street furniture (`street-details.js`)
Built before the road network, so everything that stands near a lane is checked against `roadClearance(x, z)` (city-config): the distance outside the nearest roadway as road-network.js lays its runs out (`LANE_GUIDE`: each link from merged junction centre to junction centre through its real bends, at its own width). Near merged junctions those runs stray up to ~2 m from the real centrelines `nearestRoads` measures, so a curb-outline test alone left poles in lanes. `clearOfRoads(x, z, r)` asks for `r + 0.3` m.
- **Signals (section 1).** Per junction (sites sharing a `junction` are built once), the arms of one road (within ~35° of one line: both ways, or a divided road's two roadways) form a signal group, ranked like `sidesAndGroups` (major first, then width), so group 0 should match the network's. On each arm, the corner is where its curb lines clear the crossing roads' curbs; 1.2–4.8 m past it, 0.75 or 1.3 m behind the curb, one galvanised 4.2 m pole on the right-hand curb (or a median ≥ 1.8 m wide) carries a black S-1 head (200 mm lamps, visors; centre at 3.55 m) facing the arriving traffic, and one on the left-hand curb carries a repeater on arms ≥ 12 m or 3+ lanes. Major arms get a backplate with a yellow border. Every pole also carries a pedestrian head (red standing over green walking figure, a canvas mask) at 2.45 m facing across the arm; it walks with the other group (`traffic.js` `walkOn`), steady green for `walk` s, flashing until that group's amber, red otherwise. Lens and panel geometry carry their lamp in `uv.y` (1 red, 0.5 amber, 0 green; the panel's upper half red), not in world height. No S-2 arrows, no countdowns. `audioSignals` has one entry per pedestrian head, `isAvenue` = it walks with group 0. Measured: 199 junctions, 981 poles, 510 S-1 heads, 981 pedestrian heads.
- **Name plates (1B).** For each named arm and side, a ray from the curb 3.5 m past the corner across the sidewalk finds the first wall (a `buildingColliders` box, AABB or `obb`, at least 5 m tall) within 14 m that faces the street; a white-on-blue plate ("ul. Garbary", "al. Niepodległości") goes on it at 3.3 m, one per name within 25 m. Plates come from one canvas atlas with a per-instance `plateCell`. Headless (tests) there are no colliders and no plates; with the lot rects standing in for walls, 122. The old pole blades are gone.
- **Streetlights (2).** Every curb edge takes the class of the road in front of it (`nearestRoads`): main (≥ 12.5 m, 3+ lanes or a trunk road) 10 m LED bent masts every ~34 m, side streets 7 m ones (the same mesh at 0.7) every ~30 m, both 1.08 m inside the curb with the luminaire over the road and a pooled spot. In the Old Town (Stare Miasto inside buildings.js `OLD_TOWN_BOX`) and within 650 m of the Katedra, non-main edges get cast-iron gas lanterns (ladder bar, four-sided glass) every ~22 m with pooled point lights. Measured: 1159 main, 6112 side, 1306 gas.
- **Curbside (3).** Tall red hydrants with a white band (near one end of about half the edges ≥ 30 m), Poczta Polska post boxes (8% of edges), parking machines on non-main edges in Stare Miasto, Jeżyce, Łazarz and Wilda, and bollards 7–12 m from both ends of Old Town and Stare Miasto side-street edges (clear of the crosswalk landings). All within 1 m of the curb. Measured: 2010, 268, 508, 2677.
- **Wide sidewalks (3B).** Press kiosks, flower stands and advertising columns (słupy ogłoszeniowe) 3.8 m or more behind the curb, only where the footprint stays on the block and off its lot rects (`inLot`, frames turned into place), 320 m (columns) or 420 m (kiosks) apart. Measured: 76 kiosks, 67 flower stands, 120 columns. No bike-share docks (Poznański Rower Miejski closed in 2022).
- **Cost to the network:** 15,284 footprints leave 4069 edges / 184.6 km of the bare 193.8 km (95.3%). Leaving out one kind at a time: signal poles cost 4.2 km (they keep clear of the straight runs, so probably turning curves or stop-line placement near the corners), LED poles 2.2, bollards 1.1, parking machines 0.9, the rest ≤ 0.3.

## Tram infrastructure
`tram-infra.js` builds everything static about the trams from `GEO.rail` (`kind: 'tram'` only; mainline `rail` is not drawn) and `GEO.tramStops`, after the road network. The placement is the pure `layoutTramInfrastructure(network)` (no three.js), which `tests/tram-infra.test.cjs` runs on the real network.
- **Rails:** each track is cut into straight pieces of up to 4 m (`PIECE`), so it follows bends and curbs. Each piece gets two instanced rail heads, 70 mm wide at 1435 mm gauge, and a 42 mm dark groove inside each head. On street-running track, and wherever off-street track comes within 3.5 m of a lane (level crossings), the heads are flush: top at y 0.016 and the grooves at 0.014, both above the road paint (0.012). That keeps them from z-fighting and puts them over the markings. Pieces over a curb island sit on its 0.22 m top. Other off-street track lies on a 2.9 m ballast box (top 0.14) with raised heads (0.27). The ballast shader draws concrete sleepers every 0.6 m from a per-instance `tie` attribute (piece length, arc-length phase), fading to their mean colour when they get small on screen. Pieces over water with no deck are dropped.
- **Overhead line:** a contact wire at `WIRE_Y` 5.8 m runs along every track polyline. Supports stand every ~30 m (`STATION`) along each track, unless one already carries that track within 12 m. From the track, `march` looks out on both sides for the first clear spot: on land, ≥ 2 m from every lane centreline and ≥ 1.75 m from every track. It prefers a curb, with the pole 0.6 m behind the curb edge. If no curb is found, it takes the first clear spot, which can be a centre pole between two tracks or a pole on a reservation. Where both sides are found, a street track gets two 7.6 m poles with a span wire at 6.75 m. Within 900 m of the Ratusz, streets with under 15 m between the two spots get wall rosettes 3.8 m behind the curbs instead (an approximation of the house fronts). Otherwise the nearest side within 9 m gets a pole with a cantilever arm and a stay. Off-street track only looks 5 m out. Droppers hang wherever a span or arm crosses a track. Poles join the player colliders.
- **Stops:** each stop node snaps to the nearest tram track within 25 m. Nodes within 20 m on one track merge. The platform goes on the right of the travel direction: on one-way track, the right of its points; on two-way track, the side away from a parallel track 2–8 m off (both sides are tried if there is none).
  - **Island:** a platform 1.35 m from the track centreline, 33 m long (shorter on short chains, ≥ 18 m), 2.6, 2.0 or 1.5 m wide, 0.25 m high, with a yellow tactile strip. It is curved along the track as 3 m pieces on the curb material. Every sample must be on land, ≥ 1.75 m from every lane centreline (`LANE_KEEP`; road-network's box test reaches that far on diagonal streets) and clear of other tracks.
  - **Curb stop:** if no island fits, the stop stands on a sidewalk within 12 m of the track. Passengers cross the lane to board, as at the real Poznań stops that have no island.
  - **Furniture:** a glass shelter (with a back-lit advert panel that lights by `uInteriorGlow`) when 2 m or more of depth is free, a stop sign with a name plate, and a ticket machine. The name plates come from one canvas atlas (a yellow "TRAMWAJ" band over the Polish name on ZTM green; no logos), indexed by a per-instance `plateCell`. Shelters, signs and machines join the player colliders. Platforms do not, so the player can drive and walk across them.
  - The count: 121 stops (97 islands) from 142 nodes. The rest have no track within 25 m (Katedra, Fredry, Rondo Śródka, Pl. Wielkopolski, Stare Zoo: their tracks are missing from `GEO.rail`) or no room.
- **Obstacle rule:** nothing goes to the road network. The test feeds the layout's `footprints` to `buildRoadNetwork` as obstacles and checks that the network stays almost whole. Measured: 4069 → 4065 edges, 184.6 → 184.5 km with the furniture. If you register any of it as an obstacle, re-measure.
- **Public surface:** `stops` (`{ name, x, z, heading, railIndex, s, dir, side, kind }`). `x, z` is the stop point on the track. `s` is the arc length along `GEO.rail[railIndex].pts`. `dir` is +1 when trams serving it run along the points. `heading` is their travel heading (0 = +z). `kind` is `'island'` or `'curb'`. The tram actors can dwell there.

## Buildings and collision
- `buildCityBlocks` walks the lot rects of every `BLOCKS` entry except parks (`park > 0.6`) and medians. For each rect it subtracts `LANDMARK_RESERVE`, splits deep lots (`splitDeep`), and calls `populateBlock(minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ, …, district)` on each piece. A building is a set of non-overlapping box parts `{x0, x1, z0, z1, y0, y1, roof?}`, and `commit()` emits them.
  - **Frontages and corners** come from the block's curb outline (`BLOCKS[i].poly`), not from the rect's position: `frontages()` marks a rect side as a street front (`blk.front`) when 3 of its 5 samples lie within `FRONT_REACH` (10 m) of the outline (lot rects stand 4.6 m in from the curb), or when 3 of them look along the side's outward normal at the curb within `FRONT_RAY` (18 m) with no lot rect of the block (`srcRects`) in the way (the frontage frames leave slivers of up to ~13 m uncovered behind the curb; without the ray 31% of the lot area was frontless courtyard, with it 19%), and a corner (`blk.corner`) as `'street'` where two fronts meet and the rect corner lies near the outline, `'notch'` where it doesn't. `residentialBlock` puts a corner house on each street corner, rows (`ROW_KINDS`: `kamienica`, `rynekHouse`, `villa`, `modernist1930s`, `postwarBlock`, `infill`, `low`, `gap`) along each front between them, and leaves the rest as courtyard; a rect with no front is courtyard (now and then an outbuilding). `building().street` is true only on fronts, so non-front sides get party walls.
  - **Profiles** (`blockProfile`): offices within 240 m of Bałtyk, Andersia/AND2/PFC and Collegium Altum; `CESARSKA` in a box round Zamek Cesarski, Collegium Minus, Teatr Wielki and the 1956 crosses (+160 m); `RYNEK` for rects with a front facing the `Stary Rynek` plaza (`blk.rynekSides`: probes 11 m out land inside its outline); then by osiedle (Stare Miasto splits into Old Town/Garbary/Chwaliszewo and Centrum at `OLD_TOWN_BOX`, Ostrów Tumski/Śródka within 650 m of the Katedra). Facade materials are named in `F` (four plaster colours, red and yellow brick, grey sandstone 4.4 m storeys, four Rynek pastels, three glass, and five `createPanelBlockMaterial` colours at 2.8 m storeys (`PANELS`: grey, cream and three repainted ones with accent bands) for the osiedle slabs and `postwarBlock`). Buildings are massed in whole storeys of their facade's `floorHeight` (the `storeys` list reads it from the options), so a new facade must carry one.
  - **Pitched roofs** are specs on the parts they cover, `part.roof = { shape: 'gable'|'hip'|'mansard', mat: 'tile'|'slate'|'copper', ridge: 'x'|'z' (lot-frame axis), rise }` (set by `pitch()`); the box and its collider stop at the eave (`y1`). Chimney stacks read `rise` so they clear the ridge. `commit()` turns each spec into a roofs.js roof (ridge `'z'` = `ry` π/2, since roofs.js runs a gable's ridge along `w`) unless parts stacked on the part cover over half of it (`roofCover`). The eaves overhang by `EAVE` 0.3 m across the ridge (along it too for hips, mansards and free-standing houses, never at a gable end on a party wall), the cross-section scaled so the pitch and ridge height stay.
- Irregular blocks carry frontage frames (`BLOCKS[i].frames`, from the geodata build). The generator lays the lots out in a frame turned by θ about p (`xf`), as if on a grid block. `placeInFrame` then turns everything the block emitted into place. **Any new per-block detail list must be added to the list of lists in `placeInFrame`**, or it will be misplaced on turned blocks. Pitched roofs are the exception: they carry their own yaw, so `populateBlock` takes `roofs.mark()` before the layout runs and calls `roofs.transform(mark, toWorld, xf.th)` next to `placeInFrame`.
- **Colliders** are `{ minX, maxX, minZ, maxZ, minY?, maxY, obb? }`, where a missing `minY` means 0. `obb: { cx, cz, hx, hz, cs, sn }` marks an oriented box, and min/max are then its world AABB. `pushCollider` emits exactly one collider per building part, from `y0` to `y1`.
  - Commit 49b2ef2 removed the old whole-block collider up to 26 m. It covered yards, courts and gaps, so players hit invisible walls. Make colliders follow the visible geometry, and never add coarse block-sized ones.
  - `minY` lets the player pass under raised parts.
  - main.js drops oriented colliders and those with `minY >= 3.5` from the traffic obstacles. Oriented boxes would over-block through their AABB.
  - The narrow phase is `buildingContact` in controllers.js: a circle for walkers, cyclists and the helicopter, SAT for the car. `tests/controllers.test.cjs` covers the corner cases.
- **Roofs** (`roofs.js`, `createRoofs({ materials: { tile, slate, copper }, max, parent })`): one static instance family for pitched roofs (gable, hip, mansard, gambrel), one `InstancedMesh` per unit piece × material (4 × 3), always visible under `CityBlocks`. A gable or gambrel is one stretched prism; a hip or mansard is a prism over the middle `w − d` of the ridge plus a `d/2 × d` cap at each end, so all four slopes share one pitch on any plan. `add(shape, mat, x, y, z, w, d, h, ry)` only stages a record (false when the pool of `MAX_ROOF_INSTANCES` 32k is full). For a turned frame take `mark()` before populating and call `transform(mark, toWorld, th)` after it (buildings.js `populateBlock`). `finish()` runs once (buildings.js calls it after the facade boxes), before scene culling: it writes the matrices into an `instanceMatrix` exactly `count` long, because culling skips meshes with spare capacity, and hides meshes left empty. Sloped normals rely on three's instance normal transform handling non-uniform scale. `tests/roofs.test.cjs` checks normals, pitch and counts.
- `LANDMARK_RESERVE` (buildings.js) lists the `LANDMARK_SITES` names (Ratusz, Fara, Zamek Cesarski, Bałtyk, AND2, …) whose outline, plus a 3 m margin, stays free of generic buildings. Bridge decks and `keepClearBoxes` are kept clear the same way.

## Near/far detail levels
- **Building detail streaming:** cornices, brick chimney stacks (on the eave line), skylights (flat roofs), HVAC (modern roofs only), balcony stacks, awnings, storefront signs and renovation scaffolds (green netting, or one of `WRAP_THEMES` building-wrap adverts) sit per block in a `Quadtree`. `update()` re-queries blocks within `STREAM_RADIUS` (560 m) once the focus has moved 18 m or more. It sorts them nearest first, so the nearest blocks win when a pool's fixed cap (`MAX_*`) runs out, and refills the instanced pools.
  - Uploads are limited to the used range (`flagRange` → `addUpdateRange`; commit b574893).
  - The pools are `frustumCulled = false`, which also keeps them out of scene-culling.
- **Balconies** (`createBalconies(group)` → `{ begin, add(b, focusX, focusZ), end }`, driven from the buildings `update`; the records still live in `blk.fireEscapes`):
  - A record is a stack of kamienica balconies, one per storey and no stairs: a 1.0 m deep stone slab (2.4 m wide, or the record's `w` clamped to 1.5–3 m), black wrought-iron railings on three sides and two curved console brackets.
  - Within `NEAR_DISTANCE` 90 m of the focus, each balcony is an instance of real geometry, up to `MAX_NEAR_BALCONIES` (256) in total. The `maskNode` cuts balusters, rails, the ornament band and the bracket profile; a per-instance `balconyStyle` picks the band (rings, serpentine scroll or diamond lattice), hashed from the stack position unless the record sets `style`.
  - Farther out, or once the near budget is spent, a stack is a single open box whose `maskNode` draws a slab band and a picket railing per storey from `balconySize = (storey, box height, width)`.
  - buildings.js `commit()` emits them for half the kamienice, corner houses and 1930s houses (`BALCONY_P`), on street fronts only: 1–3 stacks per front (one per ~7 m), one slab width (1.8–3 m) and railing style per building, from the first floor (the second over shops, clear of the awnings) up to six storeys. Measured: about 9000 stacks city-wide, at most ~1100 within `STREAM_RADIUS` (Łazarz) against the 2400 cap and ~145 near balconies within 90 m (Wilda) against 256.
  - The record holds `x, y, z, ry, facing, storey, landings, h` (plus optional `w`, `style`): `z` sits 0.55 m off the facade, `facing` turns local +z into the street, `y` is the lowest slab top, and `h = (landings - 1) * storey + 1`.
- **Trees** (`trees.js`): three species, by `species` index on each site: 0 lime (lipa, the default: most park and wide-street trees), 1 Norway maple (narrow streets, plaza planters, leftover slabs), 2 horse chestnut (a share of parks and wide streets). Callers pick the index (`street-details.js` section 4, `city-grid.js` park sites, `buildings.js` leftover slabs). Each species has one tile of the 2×2 leaf atlas (tile 3 is the far-canopy clump; a 4th species needs a wider atlas and its own `meshIndex` entry) and its own bark in `createTreeMaterial`, keyed off the same index. `tests/trees.test.cjs` paints the atlas on a recording canvas and checks that every path stays in its tile. Trees are procedural rows; real OSM `natural=tree` positions are not in the geodata. `LOD_NEAR` is 105 m, `LOD_MID` 420 m, then crown cards, with 6 m of hysteresis. The meshes are regrouped per 200 m cell / species / variant / level. Instance buffers use static usage on purpose: DynamicDrawUsage re-uploads in every pass of every frame.
- **Scene culling** is automatic for anything under the seven named roots; thresholds and opt-outs are in [`rendering.md`](rendering.md#culling-scene-cullingjs-registering-new-static-geometry).

## Batching
- `batchStaticGroupMeshes(root, excludeMaterials)` merges all non-instanced meshes that share a **material object** into one mesh. It keeps only `position`, `normal` and `uv`, and meshes missing any of those are skipped. The originals are removed, so their names, userData and custom attributes are lost.
  - Pass a material in `excludeMaterials` to keep its meshes separate, for example a material with custom attributes, or one whose mesh another module looks up.
  - city-grid.js batches its group at the end of `buildCityGrid`, and landmarks.js at the end of `buildLandmarks` (the `LandmarksDynamic` root is left out).
- Share material instances wherever possible. Every distinct node material is another WGSL pipeline to precompile behind the loading screen, which is why landmarks share "canonical" facade materials.
- landmarks.js re-emits unrotated `BoxGeometry` parts that use a material from `boxLocalTwins` as instanced facade boxes (`createFacadeBoxes`, tsl-materials.js).

## Recipes
**New kind of street furniture** (in `street-details.js` `buildStreetDetails`):
1. Pick sites with the local helpers: `isOnCurbIsland`, `isOnSidewalk(x, z, pad)`, `inPlaza`, `blockAt` (city-config), `isClearOfPoles`, `clearOfRoads(x, z, radius)`, and `inLot` for the property line. To place props along every real block at any bearing, walk `curbEdges` (curb edges ≥ 24 m fronting open roadway, with inward/outward normals), as the lamps and street trees do.
2. Call `addFootprint(x, z, w, d, kind)` for each prop that should block pedestrians and lanes, and mind the obstacle rule above: check `clearOfRoads`, and keep it within ~1 m of the curb or beyond 3.8 m (the walking lines run 1.7 and 2.8 m in, ±0.4 m, `pedestrian-routes.js`).
3. Build one `InstancedMesh` per geometry and material, and `group.add` it. It is chunk-culled automatically if it passes the thresholds above. Do not set `frustumCulled = false`.
4. For a night light, push a site onto `streetPointSites` (pooled point lights) or `ledLampSites` (pooled spots).
5. For player collision, return a `colliders` array from `buildStreetDetails` and add it to `allColliders` in main.js. Street details currently return no colliders, so the player passes through hydrants and the like.
6. For a sound source, follow how `audioSignals` is consumed by `CityAudioSystem`.
7. A new module needs the registration steps in `AGENTS.md`.

**New landmark:**
1. Footprint: add its OSM name to `tools/data/q_poz_landmarks.overpassql` and its curated entry to `tools/poz-extra.mjs`, refetch and rebuild ([`geodata.md`](geodata.md)). It then appears in `LANDMARK_SITES[name]`.
2. The area module (`landmarks-<area>.js`, conventions in the `landmark-kit.js` header): add a block `{ const c0 = colliders.length; …model around a local origin…; group.add(obj); collider(colliders, …); fitToSite([obj], site('Name'), colliders, c0, { height }); }`. `fitToSite` scales the model to the real height, capped by the outline width (+6%), and moves it onto the site centre. It applies the same transform to every collider pushed since `c0`, so author colliders in the model's own coordinates, as axis-aligned boxes. When the site has an `ang`, the model is scaled in the site's own frame and then turned by `ang` about the site centre, `offset` turns with it, and each collider becomes an `obb` collider (the shape buildings.js `pushCollider` emits), which keeps it out of the traffic obstacles. Turned box parts keep the world-space window grid, because the `boxLocalTwins` re-emit skips rotated parts. Reuse the shared facade materials, and use the `boxLocalTwins` keys for box parts.
3. Add the name to `LANDMARK_RESERVE` in buildings.js, or generic buildings will grow through it.
4. Optionally add it to `CITY_LANDMARKS` and `TELEPORT_LOCATIONS` (city-config.js) for labels, the map and teleports.
5. Towers outside the detail box belong in outskirts.js instead (coordinates via `LL()`).

**New streamed building detail:** add a list to `blockDetail` in `populateBlock`, fill it in `commit()`, add it to `placeInFrame`, create a capped pool next to the `MAX_*` constants, and fill it and set its count and `flagRange` in `update()`.

## Determinism
- World building uses no `Math.random`. Only traffic.js and pedestrian-routes.js do.
  - buildings.js reseeds `mulberry32` per block from `(minX, minZ)` at the start of `populateBlock`. A block's buildings therefore depend only on its own bounds and profile: editing one block leaves the others unchanged, but any geodata change to a block's rect reshuffles that block.
  - outskirts.js and trees.js use `mulberry32` with fixed seeds per generator; city-grid.js and street-details.js hash positions (`prand`, `hashPos`); bridges.js uses no randomness.
- Keep new procedural placement on a seeded generator, so screenshots and tests stay reproducible.
- Draw order matters in the shared generators. Inserting `rand()` calls into an existing loop changes everything generated after it in that seed's scope.
