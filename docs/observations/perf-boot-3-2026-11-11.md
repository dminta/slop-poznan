# Faster boot, part 3: the land raster, road-network, square and rail keep-out indexes (2026-11-12)

## Question

Part 2 ([record](perf-boot-2-2026-11-11.md)) took the headless build from 15.4 to 9.5 s. Its profile left four named
boot entries: `city-config.js`'s module import (the 2 m land raster and its `distToWater` chamfer), `buildRoadNetwork`
(`crossingRuns`, the largest-component pass, the footprint hash), `city-grid.js`'s square paving (`pavedPlazas()` road
sampling, `ringDist`, `railCells`) and the 1 m rail keep-out raster in `layoutRailInfrastructure`. This round profiles
the current build in `js/main.js` order, then narrows the candidates each predicate sees with buckets, bbox rejects,
CSR typed arrays and row spans. The arithmetic of every final test is unchanged; no world data or drawn geometry
changed.

## What changed

- **Land raster (`js/city-config.js`).** The 2 m raster over the 13.6 × 5.7 km detail box is 6,806 × 2,838 =
  19,315,428 cells. The land/hole scanline fill (13 ms), bridge capsules (7 ms) and the final `Uint8` conversion stay
  as they were; only the two-pass chamfer is restricted to 32-cell blocks within 4 blocks (127 cells) of a water block,
  because a cell farther than that cannot come out below the 255 m cap. The DP still visits every retained cell in
  row-major order with the same `Float32Array`, `Math.min`, `2` / `2√2` weights and final `Math.round`; skipped cells
  are written as 255. The section dropped from 236 to 51 ms in a fresh Node import, and `[cityConfig]` from 577 to
  470 ms in the headless phase table (the rest is the 4.6 MB geodata import and the module's other tables).
- **Squares (`js/city-grid.js` `pavedPlazas`).** Every candidate square scanned all 22,590 `ROADS` centreline segments
  for its 30 m street test, and every kept square filtered the whole road-graph segment list through `crossesPlaza`.
  Both lists now get a 32 m bucket index inside `pavedPlazas()`; a query walks the cells of the square's box, sorts
  the ids back into source order, and the unchanged `lengthInside` / `crossesPlaza` tests see that subset. The headless
  plaza section fell from 416 to 199 ms; in the final profile `pavedPlazas()` itself is 35 ms and the bucket build
  25 ms (`lengthInside`/`crossesPlaza` are then below the sampler's cutoff).
- **Park and plaza outlines (`ringDist`).** The lamp loops asked the same 300–2,000-vertex park, lawn, bed and pond
  outlines dozens of times per square; `ringDist` now builds a 16 m edge grid per ring (lazily, `WeakMap`) and expands
  rings only while the lower bound `(r - 1) · 16 m` can still beat the best edge. All call sites pass the threshold
  they compare against, so the capped return (`min(exact, limit)`) is comparison-identical. The index is released at
  the end of `buildCityGrid` (`ringDistIndexes = new WeakMap()`); a late caller rebuilds it.
- **Rail clearance (`railCells` / `railClearance`).** Each rail segment now carries its AABB, and a query rejects a
  segment whose box is farther than the best distance found (initially the cap `r`) before the projection and `hypot`.
  The 32 m bucket and the exact distance formula are unchanged; the park/plaza lamp and tree checks only look at the
  track segments that can change their answer. The headless self time fell from 308 ms in the baseline profile to
  174–197 ms in the final profiles.
- **Road network (`js/road-network.js`).**
  - `crossingRuns` rejects a whole track piece before its 1 m samples: a piece whose along/across span misses the
    edge band, or whose across span cannot reach any lane's `OPPOSING_CLEAR` band, can neither start nor keep a run.
    When it starts, all runs active at that point are closed in lane order exactly as the first sample did. In the
    headless build `crossingRuns` fell from 218 to 69 ms and the `clearOpposingTracks` section from 437 to 287 ms.
  - The generic `largestComponent` now builds CSR successor/predecessor arrays in typed arrays and uses two
    `Int32Array` stacks; successor order (and therefore component and tie order) is the old order. The pass disappeared
    from the browser profile's top 40 (1.27 % at 1× before).
  - The footprint hash is split into a curb map and an obstacle map, because every query asks for one kind only, and
    its cells are 16 m instead of 32 m. Over the 1.27 M headless queries this cut candidates from 2.27 M to 1.01 M
    (curb candidates 507 k → 154 k); the existential answers are unchanged.
- **Street-details (extra find, `inPlazaRect`).** The profile's largest remaining entry was not on the four-item list:
  the exact plaza/park outline test scanned every edge, including its `hypot`, for every one of the 61,550 park and
  plaza trees and every furniture candidate. It now uses the park block's `createPolygonIndex` z-slot ray test first,
  then a small 8 m edge grid (`nearRingEdge`) for the `<= m` edge-margin test. The result is `inside || nearest edge
  <= m`, exactly as before. `inPlazaRect` was 2.4 % of the 1× browser boot and 21 % of the headless build; it is absent
  from the profile after the change, and browser `[streetDetails]` fell 2,247 → 1,895 ms. The plaza and ring caches are
  reset at the end of the build (a late `addRuntimeSignalHeads` call rebuilds them lazily).
- **Rail keep-out raster (`js/tram-infra.js`).** `fillRect` derives the integer column range of each row from the two
  affine half-plane inequalities, scans the exact cell predicate only at the two ends of the run, and fills rows in
  64-cell tile spans. The 1 m cells, the `u`/`o` predicate and the `KEEPOUT_TILE` layout are unchanged; only cells the
  predicate would set are written. The function's headless self time fell from 218 to 73 ms.
- **Segment grids (`js/tram-infra.js`).** `segmentGrid` takes a `walk` flag: nearest-only grids (`laneGrid`,
  `deckGrid`, `levelGrid`, `endGrid`, `targetGrid`, `fenceGrid`, both `supported` grids) file each segment only in the
  cells it actually crosses, while grids whose `crossing()` output feeds the order-sensitive dropper arrays
  (`trackGrid`, `railGrid`, `tramGrid`) keep the historical bbox filing. A vertical segment on a cell boundary is
  filed in both columns; the `add` path was 310 ms in the baseline profile.

The only retained additions are the four AABB numbers per rail segment in the lazily kept `railCells()` index
(6,059 segments, about 0.2 MB of JS numbers). The ring, plaza and segment buckets are function-local and become
unreachable when their builders return; `ringDistIndexes` is explicitly reset.

## Measurement

Browser numbers use the refreshed `.agent-task/perf-harness` (headless Chrome 157, Apple-silicon Mac, 1280 × 720,
WebGPU): `perf-main.js` is the current `js/main.js` with the `__perfBoot` marker and imports production modules
directly; the runner records the `[init]` phases and a 200 µs whole-boot CDP CPU profile. Each pair is interleaved
A/B against a pristine `git archive 586fbcc` extraction served from the same HTTP server. (The final merge moved
`poznan` to `f2e4dca`, whose extra commits trim mesh geometry and release build scopes; the equivalence hashes were
re-run against a pristine `f2e4dca` and are identical. The timing tables keep the 586fbcc base so they compare one
change set, not the unrelated mesh trim.) The machine was shared; the 1× pairs ran four times (three baseline-first,
one after the change) and the 6× pairs three times (two baseline-first, one after).

## Equivalence evidence

- **Build hashes.** `git archive 586fbcc` vs this tree, both through the same headless harness, 58 hashes identical:
  buildings colliders / `footprintsByBlock` / `allBlocks` / industry structures, city-grid colliders / prop colliders /
  footprints / sidewalks / cut and road-cut plaza slabs / 61,550 tree sites / 3,511 lamps / plaza rects / sidewalk
  blocks, street-details footprints / colliders / audio signals / tree counts / vehicle heads / its road network
  (node and edge rows), the traffic network and crosswalks, all tram layout fields and rail layout fields, the full
  road-paint strip arrays and the P-22 bus matrices, the five console summaries, the land raster and the rail keep-out
  raster.
- **Land raster.** Every one of the 19,315,428 cell centres gives the same `isLand`, `isWater` and `distToWater` as
  the baseline (0 mismatches, also checked by `tests/boot-indexes-3.test.cjs` against a freshly rebuilt full chamfer).
- **Rail keep-out raster.** Every 1 m cell centre of the detail box (77,242,425 cells) gives the same
  `corridor.has` answer as the baseline.
- **Traffic.** `node tools/bench-traffic.mjs --only centrum --seed 4242 --json` is byte-identical (724 bytes, 53 cars
  at Centrum, same waits and queues).
- **Regression tests.** `tests/boot-indexes-3.test.cjs` (10 tests, ~5 s) compares every fast path with its plain scan:
  `ringDist` on real plaza/park rings and synthetic cell-line boundaries, `railClearance` on synthetic rails and
  boundaries, the walked `segmentGrid` against the bbox grid on 30,000 queries, `fillRect` against the per-cell
  predicate on rotated and axis-aligned rects, `crossingRuns` against the old per-metre loop on 120 random chains, the
  split footprint hash against a plain scan, `largestComponent` against the old Kosaraju on random graphs, `pavedPlazas()`
  with and without the road buckets, and the masked land raster against a full two-pass chamfer cell for cell. `tests/boot-indexes.test.cjs` keeps its part-2 checks; its `segmentGrid` signature marker was updated.
  `node --test tests/*.cjs` passes (531 tests, ~69 s) and `node --test tests/` passes (547 tests, ~154 s).

## Memory

The land raster still keeps only `LAND_CODE` and `LAND_DIST` (2 × 19.3 MB); the 77 MB `Float32Array` is temporary and
becomes unreachable when the import IIFE returns. The `ringDist` edge grids are explicitly dropped at the end of
`buildCityGrid`; the plaza and road buckets are locals of `pavedPlazas()` and `buildCityGrid()`. `railCells()` keeps
its 32 m segment index for runtime `railClearance`, now with four extra numbers per segment (6,059 segments, about
0.2 MB of JS heap); the 16 m footprint hash and the 8 m plaza edge grids live only for their build.

## Limits

- The browser runs were on a shared machine (load average 3–15), with other worktrees building; absolute seconds vary
  by several percent. The pairs were interleaved and repeated in both orders; the 1× pairs move −4 to −6 % and the 6×
  pairs −12 to −24 % depending on the pair, so the direction is consistent and the exact percentage is not.
- The 6× CPU throttle is a JavaScript slowdown, not a phone. Shader compile/warm-up and GPU wait are not throttled the
  same way, so the 6× split is more JS-heavy than a real low-end device.
- The headless harness is not the browser: it omits landmark, bridge and building colliders and runs modules in a `vm`
  realm with inert three.js, and it includes the live city-grid tree/lamp arrays that the real page also passes. It is
  used for the phase ratios and the equivalence hashes, not as a browser timing.
- The CPU profiler's sample stream is bursty; function shares were used to find candidates, and only functions whose
  direction the wall/phase times confirm are listed. `railCells` in particular appeared at 3.1 % in one baseline-only
  run and below the top-40 cutoff in most; its query replacement is measured by the phase and the regression test.
- The equivalence hashes cover the headless build; they do not render. The changes touch no materials, shaders,
  scene graph construction or draw order. The refreshed harness loaded the page with no console error or warning, but
  this round did not repeat the full visual browser review; the final appearance check remains a manual step.
